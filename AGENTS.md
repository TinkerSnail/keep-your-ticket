# Keep Your Ticket

A first-person photography game set at a local amusement park in the late 1990s. The player works there as a photographer, taking pictures of guests by day. After the park closes, the player is still inside.

## Mandatory session preflight

At the beginning of every new Codex session, and again whenever conversation
context is summarized or compacted, read
`.agents/skills/park-preflight/SKILL.md` completely and perform its full
preflight before planning or changing this project. Do not rely on remembered
chat context in place of the project documents.

Full description: `documentation/design.md`. The park after close: `documentation/night.md`. Platform and tooling decisions: `documentation/technical.md`. `documentation/design-archive.md` is a long-form tone reference, not a spec — read it for atmosphere questions, not for what to build, and check its supersession markers before trusting anything in it.

`documentation/` is not tracked by git. It is working material and stays local.

Working notes go in `documentation/journals/`, one file per day, named `YYYY-MM-DD.md`.

## Stack

- **Godot 4.7.x**, standard build (not .NET)
- **GDScript**
- Forward+ renderer
- Blender for art, imported as `.blend` directly
- Primary machine is macOS; Windows secondary

Search results for Godot commonly target 3.x, where many APIs differ. Verify against Godot 4 documentation before applying anything found online.

  The rim is 340m of ridge as **one welded mesh** since 2026-08-18, sampled every 2.5m into a front strip from the buried toe up to the crest and a back strip down behind it. `ParkPlan` had held the whole of it — `RIM_FOOT_X`, `RIM_CREST_X`, `RIM_PROFILE` and a `rim_crest` accessor **with no callers at all** — since the east cascade went in, so for as long as the east gate has existed it has framed a six metre monument with open sky behind it. **A tall box may not be tilted; a face may**, and the distinction is which dimension the tilt swings: the cascade's wing was 5.9m of height turned 32° and its corners moved 3.1m, where this is a slab whose length *is* the chord's, built in its own frame and never squared-up-then-rotated. `theta` is a quarter turn because `_xform` pitches about local RIGHT, which is a rotation in the y-z plane, and this slope runs in x. **It is textured since 2026-08-18, and it is the one tile in the park sized by viewing distance rather than by what it depicts.** Every other tile here is measured against an object with a real size — a 200x100 paver, a 140mm board, a 12mm chip — and made fine enough to resolve it. That reasoning gives the wrong answer for a landform: the ridge is 150 to 230m out, the park's own 3m ground tile subtends about twenty pixels at that range, and every feature in it lands under the first mip or two, so the whole tile averages to its own mean and the surface comes back exactly as flat as it started. **A texture that mips away is not a texture, it is a slower way of writing the albedo down.** So `RIM_METRES` is 48 and the structure inside it is 3 to 12m, which still spans two or three hundred pixels from the plaza. It is coarse enough to be nothing at all up close, and nothing ever is — the same condition the hollow carries. **The relief is what does the work and the albedo only keeps it company**: ±17% of albedo swing about the mean, and `RIM_RELIEF` at 40 in the normal map. That number was *not* derivable — sixteen times the ground's figure is the tile-size ratio and comes out around 14, which renders as a faint cloud, because the estimate counts the tile ratio and not the *feature* ratio, and a 12m swell over 128 pixels has a far shallower per-pixel gradient than a 5cm one over eight. It was set by looking. **The tile is isotropic on purpose**: the obvious thing to draw on a ridge is gullies running down the slope, and it is the one thing this must not do, because the face is already 34 slabs each tilted to its own angle and reads as a folded screen — parallel lines down every facet are what a folded screen is *made of*. Blotches break the plane; stripes would agree with the fold. It could not carry a direction reliably anyway: the material is world-space triplanar, which here is the only option rather than a preference — 34 overlapping slabs each in its own rotated frame would give surface UVs a different tiling rate per band and a visible break at all 33 seams — and triplanar costs you control of the in-plane direction. **The texture pass does not get to repaint it**: `_ground_material` multiplies its tint by the tile, so the tint is the palette colour divided by the tile's mean. A texture pass adds variation to a surface, and that arithmetic is what keeps the two decisions separate.

Every word of that was sound and it answered one viewing distance out of two. `RIM_FOOT_X` is 120, which **is** `TERRACE_TWO_TO_X`: the toe of the ridge begins exactly where the east hill's ground stops, ten metres from the head of the basin staircase and forty-odd from the belvedere. Anyone who walks up the climb ends nearer to this than they ever get to the plaza's own perimeter wall, looking at full aerial haze from arm's reach. Measured off `hill_j_shelf_east` it rendered 211/216/227 against a sky of 119/170/213 — **lighter than the sky it was meant to read against**, which is why it came back as a snowfield and why the east looked like six unrelated materials in one frame. **There is no fog anywhere in this project** — `daylight.gd` drives sun, sky and ambient and nothing else — so distance here is *painted*, and a painted distance can only ever be right for one standpoint. It is a land colour now, an olive-grey drier and greyer than `planting`, separating from the hill in front of it on saturation the way a real ridge does rather than on value. **What it costs is the plaza's east skyline**, which is a real trade: from the ring the ridge is a dark olive band where it used to be a pale one. The old argument's fear was a pale ridge lost against a pale horizon, and darker is *more* legible against that sky rather than less, so the plan's case for the crest height still holds — but it is a different composition and it was shot from both ends before the constant moved. Note that `_normal_from`'s strength and the material's own `normal_scale` are different quantities in different units — all of the slope is baked into the tile and the material gets 1.0.

**The rim wraps since 2026-08-21, and the plan had been asking for it since the day it was written.** `_rim`'s own docstring called the park "a breached crater, and this is the wall of it" and then built one wall — a straight north–south ridge at `RIM_CREST_X`, which does not read as a crater, it reads as a ridge, and it was the only landform on the map. It now runs the same 340m down the east, turns west behind the grove, and comes down to a headland in the water north of the pier: everything closed except the bearing the sun sets on.

  **`RIM_PROFILE` became `RIM_PATH`, and the old shape could not survive it.** A list of (z, crest) pairs describes a wall that runs north and south and nothing else; past the bend there are two crests at most values of z and none at all beyond the headland, so `rim_crest(z)` was deleted rather than adapted. `rim_samples` replaces it: Catmull-Rom through the control points — a polyline's corners are creases and this is land — subdivided fine, then **resampled by arc length**, because a curve parameter is not a distance and sampling one as though it were bunches the columns through every turn. It returns the crest in plan, its `foot`, the inward normal and the arc length; `_rim` takes the cross-section off that instead of off fixed x.

  **The order of the control points is load-bearing, and the first build was inside out.** `_rim_strip` winds assuming the columns advance one way, which the old sweep satisfied by running z from −170 up to +170. Authored south-end-first, every triangle came out backwards — which does not read as a winding bug, it reads as the rim having failed to generate. The list runs headland → north arm → south end for that reason and `inward` is `Vector2(-t.y, t.x)` to match. The assertion in `_rim_mesh` is what caught it, and it had to be moved to catch it: it probed *the lowest vertex*, which was safe while the whole ridge was footed at one height and is not now — `RIM_TOE_BURY` hangs off each column's own foot, so the deepest vertex is the headland's, two hundred metres away and pointing a different way, and toe and back share that height anyway so "lowest" could always have picked a back vertex and failed a sound mesh. It is anchored to the toe on the park's axis now, where inward is due west and always has been.

  **Three other things in `_rim` assumed a straight wall and each would have failed quietly.** `_rim_creased` found crest vertices by testing `x == RIM_CREST_X`, which past the bend matches nothing — it would have kept passing while silently covering only the east arm, so it tests membership of the crest array by millimetre key instead, and counts inward-facing against outward-facing rather than west against east. `_rim_jag` was phased *and* tapered on z: the west arm holds z near −200 for two hundred and fifty metres, so the phase would have been one constant offset along the whole of it and the taper pinned at full strength; it is phased on arc length and tapered on distance in plan from `RIM_AXIS_AT`, which keeps the jag zero on the axis where the documented crest figures are measured. And `RIM_TOE_Y` was an absolute −2, fourteen metres under the east arm's foot on terrace two and five metres *above* the ground at the headland; it is `RIM_TOE_BURY` at 14 now, relative to each column's own footing, so the east arm comes out exactly where it always did.

  **The sunset was the one thing this could not touch, and it was computed rather than eyeballed.** `daylight.gd` gives `LATITUDE_DEG` 32.75 and `DECLINATION_DEG` 20, so `cos H = −tan δ tan φ` = −0.234 and sunset is azimuth **294°** — world direction (−0.914, −0.407), camera yaw 66. From the pier head that sightline does not reach z −180 until it is 457m out and past x −568, and the arm's crest runs at −177 to −224. Shot at 19:30 from the pier head and from the promenade: open water, glitter path, no land near it. The breach holds.

  **The headland has to go under the water, and it is the first raw end this park has ever had to care about.** Both ends of the old ridge were raw cuts and that was free, because no standpoint could see either. This one is 180m from the pier and dead ahead of the promenade — the first build put a vertical wall of land across the bay with sky behind it. The path now carries two tail points whose crest dives past `WATER_TOP`, so the ridge submerges and the opaque slab hides the rest.

  **A swept profile folds wherever the sweep turns tighter than the profile reaches, and the first wrap did.** The ridge reaches `RIM_RUN` in to its foot and further again to its buried toe — 44m in total at a 45° gradient — so any stretch of crest whose radius of curvature is under that has a toe line crossing its own neighbours. It came out as a dark pleat running up the face with a V bitten out of the silhouette at its bottom: a modelling error in a landform, which is what it was. **Nothing in the data said so.** The control points were fine, the crest line smooth, the mesh well formed, the winding and the crease assertions both passing, the AABB right. The defect existed only in the *relationship* between the offset and the curvature, and neither of those was written down.
  Two separate causes were live at once and each would have been enough. **The turn was eyeballed** through three hand-placed points at 38–43m of radius against a 44m reach; it is an arc of `RIM_TURN_R` now, centred at `(RIM_CREST_X − R, −222 + R)` so it is tangent to both arms by construction and has one radius you can compare against a number. **And the interpolation was uniform Catmull-Rom on control points spaced from 16m to 85m apart**, which overshoots — it put a 27m wiggle into a stretch of the headland whose control polygon is very nearly straight, and folded the toe there for no reason visible in the source. `RIM_ALPHA` is 0.5 now, centripetal, evaluated Barry-Goldman against real knots; and the phantom end points are **reflected rather than duplicated**, because a duplicated end gives that span a zero-length chord and a zero chord raised to `RIM_ALPHA` is a zero-width knot interval that every term in the evaluation divides by. The tail's `foot` came down with it, since a shallow gradient is what makes a long toe: 20–24m of rise over a 30m run was a 53m reach, and at 30m of rise it is 44m.
  `_rim_unfolded` is the guard, and it is measured over a **±4 column stencil** rather than over neighbours — at 2.5m spacing three adjacent points are nearly collinear and the circumradius of a sliver is numerical noise, which is how the first version of this check reported a 1.5m radius on a curve that never goes below 58. Tightest column on the finished path is the headland: 58m of radius against 44m of reach, 13.7m in hand. Verified firing, not just written: tightening the turn back up produces *"the rim folds at (140.9, −187.9) — the crest turns on a 36.0m radius there and the section reaches 43.9m inward"*.

  **And `plaza_skyline.tscn` joined the boardwalk's section list**, which is what makes any of the above visible from the place it was built for. It was never there before and was right not to be: the rim was 250m east of the promenade behind both the bluff and the whole plaza, so mounting it would have cost a scene load to render nothing. It brings the observation tower at (54, −40) and the rim onto that horizon with it — checked, and from the promenade the shop frontage hides the tower, so the cost is nothing and the gain is a horizon on the one bearing the strip is actually walked along.

  **It was 34 boxes until 2026-08-18, and a ridge cannot be made of boxes.** One slab per 10m band, each laid on its own chord at its own slope angle, with a block behind it. Every artefact that made the thing read as a folded paper screen rather than as land came from that and none of it could be tuned out: a box is rigid, so its top edge is a straight horizontal line in z and a crest that rises and falls therefore *steps* at every boundary; the slabs lapped 0.4m rather than butting, which is the house rule and correct, and the lap showed as a dark line the full height of the face because the two slabs either side were at different angles and one stood proud; and each slab's bottom was cut perpendicular to its own chord, so the row of toes came out as teeth. 33 seams, and once the face was textured they were the loudest thing on it. **None of those is a bug in the band code — they are what a band is**, and finer bands only turn 33 seams into 99 fainter ones at three times the nodes. The fix is to stop having boundaries. A column of the mesh shares its vertices with its neighbour, so the sampling rate stops being a shape decision and becomes a smoothness one, and 68 nodes became 1.

**The crest is a crease and the face is not, and what holds that is the smooth group — not the strips and not the UVs.** Normals are averaged along z so the face shades as one surface; the same averaging carried across the crest rounds the ridge line into a soft hump, and the crest is the line the whole east composition is aimed at. Godot 4's `generate_normals` welds by **position and smooth group only** — it hashes a `SmoothGroupVertex`, which carries no UV at all — so emitting the front and back as two calls with different UVs does *not* separate them. Measured: built that way, 813 of the 816 vertices on the crest plane came back with a normal pointing east, which is the mean of a west-facing face at 51° and an east-facing back at 64°. Nothing about the scene said so — right node, right vertex count, right AABB, no error anywhere. `_rim_creased` is the guard, and it lives in the generator because nothing outside that file knows the crest is meant to be sharp. The winding has its own assertion, sampled at the **toe** rather than mid-array, because a crest vertex passes that test while the crease is failing.

**`coplanar_test.py` learned `ArrayMesh` in the same pass, and the reason is the census.** An unknown mesh type is dropped *silently* — the totals went 8682 → 8614 and nothing anywhere said a 340m ridge had stopped being looked at. It is registered with no flat face, which is the honest answer rather than a convenience: the whole point of the shape is that no part of it is an axis-aligned plane. Its extent comes from the `aabb` inside `_surfaces`, since it carries no size properties at all.

**Nothing is hollow any more.** It used to be — a 16m-thick slab in front and a separate block behind, with a pocket between them that no standpoint could see into. The back strip retired both: the crest is now a crease between two faces of one object rather than a line where one slab stops and another begins, so the ridge closes by construction. What is still true is the condition the hollow carried — the plaza is 150m west and 48m below the crest, the gap crops at 29m, and the only elevation in the park is a shelf that is not built, so the back slope and the raw cuts at z ±170 are unseen rather than finished. Both arms swing together, and that one difference is the whole of what separates pushing a chair from walking at this fidelity.
  **A guest pushing a buggy is the opposite shape and is deliberately not folded into it.** They walk perfectly normally and the thing they push is a prop rolling in front of them, so this keeps the ordinary walk cycle and changes only the arms — both hands go to the handle and stay there. Its branch goes *after* the wheelchair's, never inside it. Three chassis: a single, a side-by-side twin and a coach-built pram. **The child in it is geometry, not a guest** — a toddler strapped into a moving buggy is seated *and* moving, which is the case above all over again, and unlike a wheelchair user it has no independent movement to model. ### Six ways the tooling lies

Three cost real time on 2026-08-12 and the fourth on 2026-08-13. None of them is visible in the code, and all four fail by succeeding.

**`--script` compiles before autoloads are registered.** So a compile-time `preload` of any script that names an autoload — `ParkClock`, `ParkSections`, `ParkSettings` — resolves to a script that never finished compiling. The failure is silent in the worst possible way: the constant is not null, `set_script` accepts it and does nothing, `set("prop", value)` writes to a property that does not exist, and the generator emits well-formed output with the important part missing. `gen_props.gd` loads `section_gate.gd` lazily inside `_initialize` for exactly this reason, behind a guard that errors loudly. Anything a generator preloads must name no autoload at all, which is why `ParkPlan` is a plain `RefCounted` and not one. A generator can dodge it by loading late; a tool whose whole job is to drive `ParkClock` or cross a `ParkSections` seam cannot, and that is what `tools/run.tscn` is for — it is a scene, so the autoloads are already up before the tool's script is ever loaded.

**`godot --check-only --script` reports "Identifier not found" for every autoload reference in any file.** Those errors are phantoms — the real scene run resolves them. The mode cannot be used to check anything that names an autoload, and believing it sends you looking for a bug that is not there.

**Piped stdout from headless Godot is buffered until the process exits.** Reading the output file of a run that is still going tells you nothing about what it has printed. A script that failed to *parse* therefore looks exactly like a hang — the scene root has no script, Godot spins an empty main loop at full CPU, and the error only appears when you kill it. Kill it and read, or do not read at all.

**A capture run that waits on a file the last run left hands you the last run's pictures.** Every probe here writes to `user://` under a fixed name, so "wait until the last frame exists, then look" is satisfied the instant it is asked — and what comes back is a complete, well-formed, entirely stale set. It is worse than a missing file in exactly the way the other four are: it fails by succeeding. On 2026-08-18 it produced two wrong conclusions in a row off one build — a wall reported as blank when its pilasters were already in the scene file, and then the same wall reported as fixed before the fix had ever been rendered — and the tell in both cases was that the *file* said one thing and the picture said another. Delete the frames before the run, not after, and if a picture disagrees with the emitted scene believe the scene and check your wait.

**A regenerated scene always looks modified, because Godot rolls `unique_id` on every write.** One line per node, so `west_stair.tscn` shows 370 insertions and 370 deletions for a run that changed nothing at all. `git status` after a generator run therefore tells you *that* it ran and nothing about whether anything moved. To ask the real question, diff and drop those lines:

```
git diff -U0 -- scenes/ | grep -E '^[+-]' | grep -vE '^(\+\+\+|---)' | grep -v 'unique_id=' | grep -vE '^[+-]\[(sub_resource|node|ext_resource) .*id="[A-Za-z0-9_]+"\]$' | grep -vE 'SubResource\("|ExtResource\("'
```

Empty means the run was inert.

**And `ext_resource` went in on 2026-08-17, after the sub-resource fix had been written and did not cover it.** Godot rolls the `id=` on every `[ext_resource]` header and every `ExtResource("…")` that points at one, exactly as it does for sub-resources — so `east_cascade.tscn` reported 91 "real" changed lines for a generator run that added a single box, and all 91 were renumbered material and shader references. That is the same false alarm, one level further out, found the same day by the session that had just fixed the inner one. Two people were bitten by two halves of one bug within hours of each other, which is the argument for the filter being a command you copy rather than a thing you reconstruct.

**The last two filters were added on 2026-08-16 and the command lied without them.** `unique_id=` is not the only identifier Godot rolls on every write: it rolls the `id=` on every `[sub_resource]` header too, and every `SubResource("…")` that points at one. So **adding a material anywhere renumbers the sub-resources of every scene that carries materials**, and the `unique_id`-only filter reports hundreds of changed lines in scenes nothing touched. Two new materials for the cascade's niche fountain made `plaza_fountain.tscn` show 464 "real" changed lines — in a file whose every `albedo_color`, `roughness` and `metallic` was byte-identical. That is exactly the shape of a false alarm that gets somebody to go looking for how they broke the plaza's centrepiece. If a scene you did not touch still shows changes after this filter, grep the diff for `albedo_color|roughness|metallic|transform` before believing it. This is also the only cheap way to find out that **somebody else's in-flight work has been published by your run** — `gen_props.gd` writes all 14 scenes from whatever the file holds at that instant, so on a shared tree a regeneration you did for your own reasons carries every other session's uncommitted edits into the output with it. That happened twice on 2026-08-16, in both directions.

**A `vec4` shader uniform handed a `Plane` serialises to `null`.** `set_shader_parameter` accepts a `Plane` where the shader declares `vec4`, and `get_shader_parameter` hands it straight back — so the material is correct in the generator's own process and every check made there passes. It does not survive `ResourceSaver.save`: the parameter is written into the `.tscn` as `null`, `load()` returns null, and the uniform silently falls back to its declared default. The game reads the saved scene, so the value only ever exists in the one process that cannot use it. Measured, not reasoned — `Plane(1,2,3,1)` round-trips to `<null>` where `Vector4(1,2,3,1)` round-trips intact. That is how every submerged lamp on both cascades, plus the plaza fountain's `lamp_ring` and `foam_ring`, were dark in every built scene on the day they were written: eight call sites, all `Plane`, and the water was drawing its lamps exactly as designed over a uniform that was never there. **Grep the emitted scene for `= null` after adding a uniform** — but read the result properly, because Godot writes *every* uniform the material does not override as `null` too, and those are correct. Only the ones you passed a value for are the bug.

**`PortableCompressedTexture2D` drops its own pixels before you can save them.** Outside the editor the class frees the compressed buffer as soon as the GPU has the texture, because nothing at runtime is going to re-save one. So a generator that builds a texture and saves it gets `OK` back and writes a well-formed 360-byte resource with nothing in it — `load()` then returns a texture object, the material accepts it, and the surface is untextured with no error anywhere. Set `keep_compressed_buffer = true` **before** `create_from_image`. Verify by loading the file back and comparing dimensions, not by checking the byte count: a regular pattern compresses to a couple of KB and any size threshold big enough to catch the stub rejects the real thing.

Running the game itself needs `open -n -a /Applications/Godot.app --args --path <absolute path> <scene>` — `godot` straight from a shell initialises Metal and exits, because the sandboxed shell cannot hold a window open. **The path has to be absolute.** `open` launches through LaunchServices and the child does not inherit the shell's working directory, so `--path .` resolves somewhere with no project in it, Godot falls back to the project manager, and the run sits at about 2% CPU forever — which is indistinguishable from a capture that is still settling. It is also the fifth way the tooling lies: unlike a parse failure, this one idles rather than spinning, and it writes nothing to `user://logs` because it never opened the project.

## The boardwalk — built and walkable, not yet populated

Built on 2026-08-12. See `documentation/journals/2026-08-12b.md`.

The strip below the bluff, 160m of promenade against a 340m shore, with the interest at the north end so the walk has a direction. Santa Cruz for the ribbon one building deep; Santa Monica for the pier running out at right angles with the big silhouette at its head.

**The sequence is the design.** Straight west out of the tunnel and across the terrace, through the parapet, down the cascade — by flight, ramp or garden stair — into a 23m arrival court, and straight on west through a 24m opening in the frontage — and the water, the pier, the wheel and the coaster arrive at once, sideways. Three anchors, and no more until the crowd is in: the **wheel** face-on to the plaza with a queue and a booth, the **wooden coaster** closing the north end with a station you walk past and a structure you walk beside, and the **pier** out to a **pavilion** that is what the sun sets behind. **It has its own crowd and its own day.** 62 guests, a graph that is a spine rather than a network — a promenade is somewhere you walk along, not somewhere you cross — and four curves that disagree with the plaza's on purpose. The strip is at 9% of its peak at ten in the morning when the plaza is at 25%, and at 100% at seven in the evening when the plaza is falling. The day curves are `@export` on `crowd.gd` now, defaulting to the plaza's, and `tools/gen_crowd.gd` builds both crowds in one run. `tools/day_test.gd` measures both.

What the west still needs:

- **Density.** 539 nodes over 2,800m² of promenade. `design.md`'s own figure is ~670 for a section, and 57 people spread over 160m of a 17.5m-wide strip still reads sparse at the peak hour. This is the pass that matters most here, and it is furniture rather than people.
- **Nobody queues for anything.** There is a wheel with a booth and a queue rail and a coaster with a station, and the crowd walks past all of it. A queue is the obvious third population for a section with rides, and it is a *standing* one, which `guest.gd` does not model — it knows wandering and sitting.
- **Nobody treats the pier as a destination.** The wander graph has it as four more nodes. Walking out to the end and looking back is what a pier is for, and that is an errand rather than a wander — the same missing layer as "the schedule is the crowd's, not the guest's".
- **The back lane became the arrival court on 2026-08-14b** and is 23m deep rather than 7.5. Its east side is the cascade and its west side is the backs of the shops, which are still ten blank walls with a door in each — correct for a frontage one building deep, and now facing a court rather than an alley, so the blankness has further to carry.
- **The cascade does not clear the frontage from the pier**, and the drop is not why. It was halved to 3m to fit a wing-borne 1:12 on 2026-08-14b and put back to 6m the next day, so the monument is full height; it is 6m standing behind an 8–11m shop row, which crops it from anywhere on the strip. The layered shot the arches were aligned for — boardwalk entry, portal, plaza tunnel, on one axis at rising heights — exists only *through* the 24m entry gap rather than as a panorama. Nothing about the gradient is available to trade for it; the levers are the shop row's height or the monument's, and the second is spoken for by the bluff.
- ~~**Nobody in the crowd uses a wheelchair.**~~ Eight of them since 2026-08-14c, adults and children, five in the plaza and three on the boardwalk — including the family the cascade was built for and a mixed group standing at the alley mouth where the descent comes out. Seven guests push a buggy as of the same day, placed the same way: one aimed at the arch from the west, one arriving at the alley mouth. That second population is what would make a step-free route down pay for itself on an ordinary afternoon rather than an exceptional one — a park has one wheelchair to a dozen strollers. **The routing exists; a *compliant* route still does not, and the two must not be confused.** `edge_steps` says an edge has no steps on it, which is exactly what it says and nothing more — the north wing and the north run are smooth, and both fall at the flight's own gradient of about 1:1.6. That is a ramp a chair cannot climb unaided, and the 1:12 that would have been one was rejected with the whole bay design on 2026-08-15. So the graph now routes wheels away from steps and does not claim the result is accessible; anybody adding a real 1:12 has a graph ready to carry it. **Both were built on 2026-08-18, and the east is where they could finally be checked.** `crowd.gd` carries `edge_steps` — one byte per edge pair — plus a step-free adjacency and a step-free depth sweep beside the ordinary ones, and `nearest_node`, `route_from` and `_path_to_entry` all take the wheels into account. `guest.gd` answers `is_wheeled()` once for both populations: a wheelchair user and somebody pushing a pram are opposite shapes to animate and the same guest to a graph.
  **It could not have been built sooner and would have been scaffolding if it had.** Until the east climb there was no edge anywhere with a step-free twin, so the flag would have had nothing to bite on. The cascade and the staircase each carry a ramp on the north and a stair on the south between the same two points — 7 of the terraces' 77 edges are stepped, and the chair and buggy groups placed at the foot are the first guests in the park whose route differs from the person beside them.
  `nearest_node`'s wheeled guard is the part worth remembering: nearest-by-distance will happily snap a wheelchair onto the middle of a garden stair, where it then has no edge to leave by and stands still for ever. Building one before a graph reaches the cascade would be scaffolding nothing could check — the same argument the claim ticket is waiting on. See `documentation/journals/2026-08-14c.md`.
- **The overlook terrace is still the boardwalk's midway, and still bare.** A midway is a corridor, not a section, so the walk from the plaza gap out to the section proper gets games and food fronting both sides, one building deep. See "A midway is a corridor, not a section" in `documentation/design.md`.
- **Four sections are named and not massed.** `ParkPlan.SECTION_GROUND` has footprints for the grove, the frontier street, kiddieland and the fairground; nothing draws them yet. They are for silhouette — the park reading as continuing in five directions from inside the plaza — not for walking into.

The design's structure points at one spoke at a time — the plaza is the hub, and the park grows by gaining a section rather than by the plaza sprawling. `documentation/night.md` holds the night design and is deliberately not scheduled.

**The reference parks were checked properly on 2026-08-11** — period maps and satellite for Disneyland, Six Flags Over Texas and Knott's, images in `documentation/reference/`. Three things there bind implementation: the park takes **Disneyland's legibility with Knott's density**; **a section is a three-plaza job**, about 670 props at the plaza's density of one per 30m², which is the real reason for one spoke at a time; and **the hub is a junction, not a destination** — Disneyland's Central Plaza is 60m, and something belongs between the gate and the hub, because gate-straight-into-plaza is the one arrangement no reference uses. **The plaza went to 104m on 2026-08-13 in deliberate departure from the first half of that**, because it could not hold a peak cast of 56 plus the furniture that says what it is; the second half still stands and the entrance street is what satisfies it. The cost was enclosure: 8–12m buildings across 104m subtended about 15° from the hub where 5–9m across 80m subtended 23°, so it read more as an open square than a room. **That lever was pulled the same day** — the perimeter went to 13–19m, which puts the subtense back at 23° and the plaza back to being a room. What the taller wall cost in turn was blankness — a 19m greybox facade is a lot of nothing — and `plaza_frontage.tscn` is the answer to that, laid the same day. The park layout section of `documentation/design.md` carries the detail.

## Constraints that affect implementation

**Collecting and exploring are the primary objectives.** Density of things to find beats systems. Job tasks and minigames are texture and pacing, and stay small.

**Free aim.** The player points the camera anywhere and composes every shot. Nothing frames, snaps, aligns, or scores it, and no shot is ever confirmed as the right one.

This is a constraint on the *game*, not on the park. The park is free to point at things and does — the shot list sends the player somewhere specific and says what to look at, and the picture-spot signs advertise the park's own best angles. Both are diegetic suggestions with no obligation and nothing that completes, and both exist partly to be looked away from. What is prohibited is the game composing on the player's behalf.

**The crowd scales linearly, and it is measured rather than assumed.** `perf_test.gd` exists because a three-tier scheme — cone-of-view guests, cheap peripheral walkers, a `MultiMeshInstance3D` fill — was designed on paper for a cost that turned out not to exist. Guests were never expensive; *pairs* were. Separation walked the whole live list for every live guest, which was 87% of the crowd's frame cost at 148 people, and bucketing them by ground cell took the peak from 8.34ms to 2.52ms and made the per-guest cost flat at about 16µs from 46 people to 148. A real peak for this plaza is estimated at 320 to 640 — one person per 5 to 20m², against the current cast of 56 at one per 114m² — and at a steady 16µs that projects to roughly 5ms rather than the 36ms the old curve predicted. **Do not build a tiering system without re-measuring first.** The numbers above are simulation only; what 150 bodies cost to *draw* has never been measured.

**A stair in this project is a ramp with treads laid over it, and the ramp is wider than it looks.** `CharacterBody3D` has no step-up, so the floor of a flight is a slab whose top face sits on the nosing line. That line runs from the back of one tread to the front of the next, so over each tread the slab stands up to a full riser *above* it — at equal widths it hides every step and the player walks on a smooth slope. Make the ramp narrower than the treads so they show past it, and give each nosing a strip a few centimetres proud. Both were missing until 2026-08-14 and neither flight in the game had ever read as a stair.

**A tread topped at its upper end stands a full riser proud of the ramp, and whether that matters is a function of the riser.** `_wing_treads` tops each tread at the *upper* end of its own segment, which puts it `riser − 1cm` above the slab everywhere except where they meet. The west's wings survive that at `WING_RISE`; the east climb used `FLIGHT_RISE` and it was a 24cm wall that blocked all four ascending flights on both strips. Topped at the *lower* end instead, the tread is at the ramp where they meet and under it everywhere else, so it shows past the ramp's sides and never in its path. The rule is the same rule the rotated-box entry states — a constraint written for one scale applies at every scale — and this is the third time it has been filed under the case that found it rather than the rule.

**Once a walk test crosses a seam, everything after it is measuring a freed section.** The east gate's pier probes stood on the gap's own centre line, which became the inside of `cross_terraces` the day the seam went in. They tripped the swap, the plaza was freed, and the body fell through ground that no longer existed — reported as `FELL` with `hit: nothing`, which reads exactly like a hole in the world and is the section machinery working. Worse, it poisoned every leg *after* it: three north-west threshold legs failed in a run that had nothing to do with them. A leg that fails far from your change is worth checking against the seams before it is believed. `walk_test` walks up to both crossings and stops; `section_test` owns both, in both directions.

**A table only one consumer reads goes stale silently, and the consumer is where it surfaces.** `PLAZA_MASSES` describes the plaza's walls for the crowd's walkability check and nothing else. The east gate was cut in the geometry on 2026-08-17 and the table's two east runs still met at z +2.0 with no gap in them — a solid wall across an opening that had existed for a day. It could not have been noticed, because the crowd's graph stopped eleven metres short of that wall; the first node ever put out there was blocked by masonry that is not in the world. When a hand-authored table shadows generated geometry, ask which consumer would notice if it drifted, and whether that consumer currently goes anywhere near it.

**A guest walks on the floor, and for a fortnight every floor was one plane.** `guest.gd` flattens `to_target` before it steers — correctly, because a climb should not read as slower ground — and then never took the height at all, so a guest kept whatever y they were put down at for their whole visit. The plaza and the boardwalk are each one level, so nothing showed for as long as those were the only sections anybody stood in; the terraces climb twelve metres, and the first crowd up there walked horizontally through the cascade's flights instead of up them. **A section with relief in it breaks every assumption that was free while the ground was flat**, and the height was only the first of four: `_home_point` scattered a spawn by most of a metre and kept the node's height, `_guest` in `gen_crowd.gd` authored every body at the section's `_floor`, and `_home_point` also rolled any node at all for a *wheeled* guest — who, put down on the south garden stair where no step-free edge reaches, then walked thirteen metres diagonally through the flights to the nearest node they could use. That last one is the same guard `nearest_node` already carried, in the one other place that picks a node.

**The height comes off the leg being walked, never off the nearest edge in the graph.** Nearest-edge is what `climb_test` asserts with and it must not be what a guest walks on: the cascade's wings are a hairpin whose two legs pass 4m apart with 3m of height between them, so the line down the middle of them is a cliff in that field and a metre of lateral drift across it would teleport somebody. Three further things about the leg are load-bearing and each was measured rather than reasoned. It is **absolute, not accumulated** — taking a share of the remaining climb each frame ratchets, because `_apply_separation` shoves people horizontally without touching y, so a guest pushed back down a slope keeps the height they gained and climbs again from there (1.33m on a leg that rises 1.5). Its start is **the node behind it, not the guest's own y** — a guest who left a flight a hand low then walked a *level* terrace a hand low, because a flat leg from a low start is a flat leg at the wrong height. And progress is measured **along** the leg rather than as distance to its end, or a lateral metre counts as a metre of the walk and anybody standing to one side of a descent reads as further from the bottom than they are.

**A follower has no leg**, so they take the height off the nearest point on their leader's trail — ground the leader actually walked. Not the station they are walking to: that is up to 2.4m back along the path and they stop half a metre short of it, which on a flight was most of the 1.33m. What is left is the one case `climb_test`'s tolerance is loose for, and it is worth knowing as the floor on how tight that can be made: a follower keeping station a metre to the side of somebody climbing can have their nearest trail point most of a stride further up, so they stand at the height of the tread beside them. 0.58m, once, in 16,800 samples.

**The west cascade has never had any of this**, because no crowd graph reaches the descent — the plaza's stops at x −31 and the boardwalk's starts at −74, both single-plane. It is the same monument and it is not the same bug; it will become one the day a route goes down it.

**A route the player can walk is a route that has been walked.** `CharacterBody3D` has no step-up, coplanar surfaces snag, and a gap behind a platform is a hole somebody falls into and cannot climb out of. None of that shows in a screenshot. Anything new that the player is meant to traverse gets driven with a scripted walk, both directions, plus a probe at every open edge.

**A waypoint the player can walk past is a test that lies both ways.** The two `wing -> court` legs aimed at a point 22m away in z and 4m in x — a diagonal so shallow the body walks past the arrival radius without ever entering it, then runs out its clock. It passed once and timed out twice on *identical* geometry, and the first fix went into the rails, which were innocent. A leg that fails intermittently is reporting on its own waypoint, not on the world; aim it along the thing it is asking about.

**And a route that moves between sections has to move between the tests with it.** The west stair was walked in `walk_test`'s plaza phase, and on 2026-08-14 the seam moved to the arch: `section_test` stopped walking the stair the same morning, and `walk_test`'s boardwalk legs began at an arrival point nobody is put down at any more. The descent stopped being covered on the exact day it stopped being *possible* — the boardwalk's own notch-fill had been sitting in the well the whole time, harmless while the stair was mounted elsewhere and a wall across the route once it was not. When a seam moves, ask which test owned each side of it.

**A list of coordinates with nothing asserting they mean anything will drift, and drifting is invisible.** `gen_crowd.gd`'s plaza POIs — the things guests turn their heads to look at — were sixteen literals in the *80m* plaza's coordinates, never put through `ParkPlan.plaza_out` when the plaza grew on 2026-08-13. "The photo hut counter" was 0.5m from a bench on the fountain skirt and 17m from the hut. Three more (the wheel, the observation tower, the coaster) were 27–37m from the nearest walkable point against `poi_near`'s **nine metre** reach, so they had never once been selected. The fountain's own entry was unreachable for the same reason: the reach is 9m and `FOUNTAIN_RADIUS` is 9. Fixed on 2026-08-14c — every entry is now derived from plan data or from the same `plaza_out` + `clear_of_walkways` pipeline the props went through, and `_reachable_pois` drops and *prints* anything nothing can get to. Measuring reach against the graph alone is the trap: it calls the fountain's jets unreachable, because the ring walkway keeps 15.6m off the middle, and the seats are where the people are.

**Is this argument a world position, or an offset from one?** Three separate bugs on 2026-08-14c were that question answered wrongly, and none of them showed up as an error. `_sphere` took a finished position where `_box` and `_cyl` take base-plus-local, so it alone ignored the plaza dilation and left three balloons 5–13m from their strings. `_cafe` put a position that was *already* final through `plaza_out` as well, moving the terrace 14m into a shopfront while the guests stayed put. And `_wing_treads` passed a world point as the *local* offset with a `Vector3.ZERO` base, so `_place` rotated it by the leg's `theta` — fine at 0 on the outbound leg, and a negation at pi on the return one, which built the south wing's twelve treads 127m away on the far side of the park. **A cheap general check catches the whole family: assert that a scene's contents are where that scene lives.** Everything in `west_stair.tscn` belongs at x < 0; one line of arithmetic over the emitted text found the twenty-five nodes that were not, once anybody thought to ask.

**Two helpers that build parts of the same assembly have to compose position the same way.** `_box` and `_cyl` in `gen_props.gd` take a base and a local offset and put them through `_place`, which is what applies `ParkPlan.plaza_out`; `_sphere` took a finished world position and did not, so it was the one primitive that silently ignored `_dilate_plaza`. Every caller outside the plaza was unaffected — out there the map is the identity — and the three inside it were the balloons, which came out 5.4m, 5.6m and 13.2m from their own strings. Nothing catches that: `coplanar_test.py` has no opinion about shapes that have drifted *apart*, and a plaza with a balloon in the wrong half of it photographs as a plaza with a balloon in it. The signature is the invariant. Fixed on 2026-08-14c, and `tools/_balloon_probe.gd` is the check.

**And a thing a guest carries inherits whatever the skeleton does to the limb it hangs off.** The same balloons, carried: parented to `arm_r`, which `_animate` swings 25° each way and `_apply_seated_pose` parks at a fixed 20°, so a 1.4m string leaned by up to two thirds of a metre and read as a loose balloon drifting over the plaza. Most carried things *should* be rigid to the arm — a cup, a map, a plush. A balloon is the exception, because string is not rigid, and it now hangs off its own knot node that `guest.gd` counter-rotates. The correction has to be at the *hand*: rotating the string about its own centre swings its top end out of the fist.

**A follower is not on the graph, so nothing that validates the graph protects it.** `_validate_graph` checks every walkable edge against the fountain and passes; the guests found walking through the fountain were the ones *not using* it. A follower has no route and no waypoints — it steered straight at its leader's current position, and a straight line to a moving point is a chord. Two separate causes and both were needed: followers now walk the leader's own breadcrumb trail rather than at the leader, so they cannot cut a corner the leader did not cut; and `crowd.gd::_admit` puts a group down *together*, because `_home_point` rolls an independent graph node per seatless guest and was scattering groups across the plaza on every clock jump, leaving followers to beeline 21m home across the pool. `tools/inpool_test.gd` is the check — a test rather than a probe, because it asserts rather than photographs, and it went green — and it samples for twelve seconds a clock rather than once — the single-instant version reported three offenders one run and none the next, which can prove a bug exists and can never show it gone.

**A kind vocabulary that grows breaks every `==` written against it.** `follow_offset` chose a follower's trailing distance with `kinds[i] == "adult"`, and there are seven kinds — so `chair_adult`, `stroller_adult`, `twin_adult` and `pram_adult` all fell through to the *child's* range and every wheelchair user was parked up to 2.4m behind their own group. It reads as somebody being left behind and no amount of pace-matching touches it: `_pace_group` was already making them faster than their leader, so they were closing on a station that was two metres back. `_guest`'s doc states the convention — `begins_with` for the prefix, `ends_with("kid")` for the age — and that line used neither. **And a branch in `gen_crowd.gd` must draw the same *number* of `_rng` values whichever way it goes**, or every guest generated after it shifts; different ranges are free, different counts are not.

**A still frame cannot show a direction, and it will look correct either way.** `water_fall.gdshader` ran its travel term backwards from the day it was written — `- TIME * flow` where a band travels at `dy/dTIME = +flow/grain`, so the sheets climbed their own lip and the jets rained into their nozzles. Four rounds of screenshots went past it, because every still of water going up is a still of water going down: the pattern, the break-up and the brightness are identical and only the direction differs. Anything that *moves* gets checked by capturing consecutive frames and measuring the shift between them — `tools/flow_probe.gd` and `tools/flow_probe.py`, the same trick `menu_capture.gd` uses for a tab change. Correlating raw frames finds nothing: the static scene outweighs the moving part, so subtract the per-frame mean first, crop to the width of the thing that moves, and keep the frame gap under half the pattern's spacing or the match wraps and the sign flips.

**A material carrying a world position is not shared material — it is a material for one place.** `water_pool` takes a `centre` uniform and radiates its rings from it, and `water_fall` takes `fade_from`/`fade_to` in absolute world Y. Both were written for a fountain standing at the origin of a plaza at y=0, where every number in them happened to double as a local one. Reused sixty metres west and six metres down, the rings arrive as parallel stripes travelling in one direction — brushed metal, not water — and the whole fade band sits above the geometry wearing it. The cascade's niche fountain therefore has four water materials of its own, and the ones it *does* share are the two `StandardMaterial3D` stones, which carry no coordinates at all. **The tell is in the uniform list, not in the render**: a still frame of stripes is a still frame of rings seen close up, which is the same blind spot the flow direction sat in. Before reusing a `ShaderMaterial`, read its parameters and ask which of them are positions.

**A setback written as a length has to be re-checked every time the thing it is set back from changes size, and nobody ever does.** Three reports from the cascade in one sitting were all this: the landing fill's west face written as `wx` when it meant `NICHE_DEEP + 0.2`; the planted bank laid to `wing_path[1]` when the turn landing reaches 0.9m past that vertex; and the return leg's handrail cut back a flat 3.0m, written when a leg was long, still 3.0 after `LANDING_HALF_W` went 7.0 → 3.5 and the leg came to 4.8 — so the rail ran 1.8m and quit, unguarding the steepest end of the descent. **Write the relationship, not the number.** The rail stops where the drop it guards falls under `RAIL_FREEBOARD`, which is a height, so it tracks the gradient; the bank runs to the landing's own `out_z`. And note what did *not* catch any of the three: `walk_test` asks whether a guard is in the way, never whether it is there, and `coplanar_test` has no opinion about a thing that is missing. All three needed somebody walking the route — which is the argument for shooting a new assembly from the standpoints the park delivers the player to, not only from the one it is composed for.

**A position that moved by decision must not also be moved by the map.** `ParkPlan.PLAZA_CAFE` holds the terrace in final coordinates, and `gen_props._cafe` ran it through `plaza_out` as well — radius 26.8 became 40.8, four metres inside the perimeter shopfronts, so every table and umbrella stood in a building. It broke exactly one of the three files that read the constant: `gen_crowd.gd` does not dilate it, so the guests and the walkability validator both stayed correct and disagreed only with the file that draws the thing. The symptom was seven guests sitting on thin air with their chairs fourteen metres away. `_queue` states the rule for the photo hut and the terrace is the same case. `tools/seat_test.py` is the check — every guest with a `seat_at` must have a seat surface under them, with the fountain's coping counted as one and wheelchair users excluded, since they bring their own.

**A screenshot is the only thing that can check what a threshold frames.** The complement of the rule above, and it earned its place twice on the same doorway: the door at the foot of the stair sat level with the hole in the boardwalk's frontage, so standing inside the well you saw the deck, the crowd and the water through it and the alley's reveal had already fired. No test has an opinion about that. Both times it was caught by a shot taken through the opening, which is why `west_capture.gd` now holds one permanently.

**An opening's clear height is not what it crops by — its depth is.** The west arch had a 5m soffit over 13.5m of tube, and 5m sounds generous next to a person. What limits the view through a *deep* opening is the far edge, and from the ring's west vertex that edge stood 28m off, which put the ceiling on a 6.7° ray: the wheel's rim tops at 25.8m and stands 87m on from that same vertex, so the ray reached y=12 by the time it got there and most of the west's biggest silhouette was behind masonry from **everywhere in the plaza**. That 87m read "past the mouth" here until 2026-08-16b, which is the *right* number against the wrong datum: a ray starts at the eye, so the distance it has to be measured over is the one from the standpoint, and past the mouth the wheel is 72.5m. Worth its own line because it fails differently from the derived-height trap below — nothing about it is reconstructed, the arithmetic it feeds is correct, and the only thing wrong is where it is measured from. A distance with no stated origin is not a fact yet. Nothing showed it. The plaza's own comment argued the through-view survived the narrowing and was right — it had checked the *lateral* cone, where the pavilion and the pier fit whole and always had. Height was never checked because a soffit reads as a ceiling, and a ceiling reads as something you are under rather than something you are looking through. When an aperture gets deeper, the crop it applies gets tighter at both edges by the same arithmetic; measure the far one. A member at the *near* plane costs almost nothing by comparison — the beam that replaced the lintel is higher at 6.3m and 18.5m closer, and the rim now reads whole from `ParkPlan.ARCH_RIM_CLEAR_X` inward.

**Measure the thing you are framing; do not derive it.** The paragraph above said "21.9m" for most of 2026-08-16, in `AGENTS.md`, `ParkPlan` and `west_capture.gd` at once, and it was wrong by 3.9m. It came from assuming the wheel sits 1.5m above `SHORE_TOP` and adding two radii, when the generator had already written hub 12.604 and outer radius 13.2 into `west_far.tscn`, a file that takes one command to read. `ParkPlan.WHEEL_TOP` publishes it now, so the arch reads the wheel's height instead of reconstructing it. Every downstream number inherited it: a margin quoted as "four metres in hand" was really a 0.9m *shortfall*, and the pose written to catch a beam creeping down was pointed somewhere it could not have caught it. Three further traps rode along, and each one turned a number somebody had quoted as a fact into a function of conditions they had not stated. The camera is **not** at the pose's `pos` — the spring arm is 2.6m and puts it about 2.5m back, which is further from the aperture and tighter than a standing eye, so sizing anything off `pos` overstates the clearance. A margin is a property of a *standpoint*, not of a design: quoting one number for "the margin" is the same error as checking one height on a pose applied to a range. **And it is a property of the pitch, which runs the wrong way.** The arm *hangs* the camera, so pitching up swings it down — measured in the running game, the eye at `ARCH_RIM_CLEAR_X` sits at y 1.47 at 9°, 1.22 at 11°, 1.10 at 14°, against the 1.55 this paragraph asserted until it was checked. A lower eye needs a lower soffit to clear the same rim, so the requirement at that pose falls from 6.11 to 5.81 across that range against a built 6.298 — every one of them clears, by 0.19m at 9° and 0.49m at 14°. Extrapolate the other way and it crops: an eye at 1.75 would want 6.34. So anyone who opens `01b`, decides the framing is untidy and nudges the pitch up has made the shot **easier to pass**. The tidying instinct and the sensitivity pull opposite ways, which is why the pose is shot at its own pitch, unadjusted. Print the camera's actual `global_position` rather than trusting the pose; all three of these were caught that way and none of them any other. `_wheel` in `gen_props.gd` owns the wheel's geometry and publishes nothing, which is why this was derivable in the first place — if you find yourself reconstructing a dimension that some other generator already fixed, open its output.

**A table that does not say which rows were measured is worse than a loose number.** The pitch trap above took two passes to write down correctly, and the second error was not arithmetic. One person produced a table of camera heights against required soffits, mixing three *observed* rows with a fourth extrapolated upward to show where the shot would crop; the other read it keyed on pitch rather than on height, took the extremes, and published a span running from the illustrative row to the middle of the measured ones. That span said the pose crops at 9°, when it clears by 0.19m — so anyone who nudged the pitch down and then read this file would have gone hunting for a regression that did not exist, which is precisely the failure the entry was written to prevent. Neither reading was unreasonable. The table just did not carry which rows were observed and which were invented, and nothing about a table announces that it is missing. **Mark the observed rows as observed.** A scalar without its conditions is dangerous because it is visibly incomplete; a table without them is worse, because it looks complete.

**A cheap twin may be cheaper. It may not be a different shape.** `west_far.tscn`'s wheel and `boardwalk.tscn`'s shared one `_wheel` call for the ring, hub, spokes and legs — and the section then built a deck under it and hung eight cars on the rim, while the tableau got neither. So the wheel the plaza looks at all day stood 0.6m low and was a bare hoop, and the one you walk up to has cars on it. That is not a fidelity difference, it is a different ride. The tableau's whole job is to be *recognisable* at 87m, eight cars are most of what says "wheel", and they cost eight boxes. `_wheel` builds the entire machine now — deck, ring, hub, spokes, legs, cars — and the two callers differ only in palette; what genuinely does not read from the far side stays with the section, because the fence, the queue rail and the booth are 1.2m tall behind an 8m frontage. The internal tell had been sitting there for days: the tableau's 24 night rim bulbs were written at the *real* hub height, so they had been orbiting 0.6m above the ring they trace. **And the haze band is not free either.** The cars went in as `far_shade`/`far_warm` first, on the argument that the three-tone wash is what makes the west read as distance — and shot from one camera on both sides of the seam they were simply not there. Grey beads on a grey ring at 87m are a grey ring. What *identifies* a thing has to be the same colour from both sides of a seam, which is the rule the pavilion's night lighting had already reached from the other direction.

**A screenshot cannot check anything below the knee, and it will say it can.** The complement of the rule above, and it cost four rounds of looking at pictures on 2026-08-14c. A wheelchair guest's feet sat three centimetres *through* the footplate and every screenshot looked right, because a screenshot of a seated guest is taken from standing and the footplate is the one part a standing camera cannot see. What found it was arithmetic across the whole 1.05–1.9m height range, and then a measurement taken in the running game. A pose applied to a range gets checked over the range, not at one height and by eye — and if the part in question is a rotated box, its lowest point is a corner, which moves with both the sine and the cosine of the angle. Solving for the joint and hanging the geometry off it is the error that looks correct.

**A constraint written for one scale applies at every scale, and it will be filed under the case that found it.** The rotated-box rule immediately above was written on 2026-08-14c about a wheelchair footplate: three centimetres of error on a part the size of a hand. On 2026-08-16 the cascade's wing was one 5.9m box tilted 32° to the slope, which swings its corners 3.1m along the run, so each leg overshot both ends and the outbound legs' top corners came through the arched opening. Same rule, hundredfold scale, and nobody thought to point a note about a footplate at a retaining wall. When a rule earns its place, the incident goes in the entry and the rule goes in the title — and then read the titles, not the incidents. **The reason it was hard to see is worth its own line: it was correct output from wrong intent.** Every box sat exactly where its own transform said, nothing was malformed, nothing was NaN, and the coplanar pairs it threw were a downstream symptom rather than the fault. Measuring the emitted geometry therefore returns true statements that mean nothing — a fault of this kind is only visible against the shape somebody meant to build, which is why the person holding a live shape is the only one who can diagnose it, and why the right response to a fight in somebody else's in-flight work is to report it and stop. Every intermediate measurement taken of that wing from outside had a shelf life of minutes; it went from seven pairs to zero in twenty, untouched.

**A wall can be shorter than it is thick, and "the longer side is the length" is not a rule.** `_facade` decided which way a perimeter run faces with `size.x > size.z`, and it was right fourteen times out of fifteen. `perim_e_mid_n` is 11m deep and 10.4m long, because the east gate ate most of it — so the run was turned ninety degrees and its entire frontage was built on its north *end* face, which is buried inside `east_pier_north`. It had been like that since the gate was cut, and it was invisible from inside the plaza in the only way that matters: a wall with nothing on it looks like a wall nobody has got to yet. `_perimeter_depth` is the fix and it is derived rather than written down — a perimeter is one wall of one thickness, so the depth is the horizontal dimension the fifteen runs agree on and the other one is the length. A mode and not a minimum, because `perim_s_west` is 11.7 by 11 and any tolerance wide enough to catch the broken run turns that corner block instead.

**An offset the seam ring can eat is not an offset.** `EAST_FAR_NUDGE` stands the terraces' massing copy of the plaza clear of the boardwalk's, and it was 6mm against a ring whose full swing is `SEAM_STEP * SEAM_STEPS` — 5.25mm. Two scenes' displacements pulling opposite ways cancelled it to two *microns*, and `coplanar_test` reported 15m² between a massing wall and a shopfront mouth eighty metres away, in two files neither of which had been edited. The instinct is to raise it past the ring, and that is where the second half of the lesson is: it cannot also be what keeps a massing box off `plaza_frontage.tscn`, at any value. Every frontage face sits at a wall's inner face plus some multiple of 5mm, and the ring's swing is *wider than that pitch* — so for any nudge there is a frontage plane the ring can walk onto. Raising it to 37mm duly moved one pair and created another sixty metres away. What carries that separation is `_mass_thinned`, which halves every far run's depth with its outer face left where it was, putting the inner face five and a half metres inside the wall: a hundred times any offset the frontage uses. **When a separation has to hold across two scenes, move the face out of reach rather than nudging it** — the ring is a within-scene mechanism and has never claimed otherwise.

**Anything two generators both need to know goes in `ParkPlan`.** The cafe's three tables were a literal typed into `gen_props.gd`, again into `gen_crowd.gd`'s chair mirror, and a third time into its walkability obstacle list — so moving the terrace updated two of the three and left the validator reporting a new street as blocked by a table that no longer existed. `ParkPlan.PLAZA_CAFE` is the one copy now. Positions are layout, and layout goes in the plan.

**And a route in `ParkPlan.WALKWAYS` is a claim that has to be checked against the geometry.** Three of the four threshold spokes ran straight through buildings — `spoke_ne` for 21m of `perim_e_north` — for as long as the only consumer was a minimap, where a line through a wall is a few pixels of nothing. Paving them showed it in one screenshot. `walk_test.gd` did not catch it either, because every threshold leg started *at* the passage mouth: it tested four passages nothing could reach. It now walks each spoke from the ring first. If a walkway is added or moved, walk it.

**A prop's written position is not where it stands, and only half of them were ever checked.** Most plaza props are typed in the *old 80m* coordinates and run through `ParkPlan.plaza_out`, so a coordinate that cleared a walkway before 2026-08-13 has no reason to now — and 32 of them did not, including five benches and four bins in the ring walkway and a picture-spot sign dead on the entrance axis. The scattered ones were fine the whole time, because `open_spots` rejection-samples against `walkway_clearance`; the hand-placed ones had no equivalent. **`ParkPlan.clear_of_walkways` is that equivalent**: an assembly's base is dilated and then pushed to the nearest place off the paving, so the authored position stays the *intent* and clearing the walk is a rule. It is a pure function of one point, deliberately — `gen_crowd.gd` has to reach the same answer without sharing a register — which means it cannot see what is already standing there, so `tools/clearance_test.gd` checks both: nothing in a walkway, and nothing in another prop. Two props pushed off the same paved edge is a real failure mode and it happened four times the first run. Props that belong to a *building* are not pushed — the bandstand's benches gave up a bearing instead, because a bench moved to the nearest clear metre stops being one of a ring of three.

**Shapes may overlap; they may not share a plane.** Overlapping is deliberate — a coplanar butt leaves a zero-width seam for the capsule to catch on, so parts run into each other rather than meeting edge to edge. But two faces pointing the same way at the same depth z-fight, and that reads in play as the ground vibrating. The generator hands every shape a fraction of a millimetre of displacement in build order so no two things that meet can also line up; anything hand-authored gives way downward and inward, never up or proud. `tools/coplanar_test.py` enforces it. Like the walk test, this exists because a still screenshot cannot show it.

**And you cannot clear a fight by re-ordering the build.** The displacement is handed out in build order and wraps at 21 (`SEAM_STEPS`), so a shape's hair is its ordinal mod 21 — which means changing how many nodes a scene emits *before* some other shape moves that shape's displacement, and moves it cyclically. Growing the wheel from twelve nodes to twenty-one on 2026-08-16 pushed the far coaster's columns exactly one wrap on and put `coaster_col_0`'s underside on `front_arcade`'s: eighty metres from the edit, in two shapes nobody had touched. The obvious repair is to move the offending call so everything after it shifts back, and it does not work while looking exactly as though it should — moving `_wheel` to the end of `_west_far` took twelve nodes out from before the coaster, and −12 ≡ +9 (mod 21), so the identical pair reproduced. **Fix the shape, not the order.** The real fault was a column bottomed at exactly `SHORE_TOP`, which shares its underside with every building standing on the same shore — an overlap two hundred nodes away in build order, which is precisely the case the ordinal cannot protect and never claimed to. `COASTER_EMBED` foots them half a metre into the ground, which is what real footings do and what a coincidence cannot survive.

**A generator run is not a private act.** `gen_props.gd` writes all fourteen scenes from whatever the file holds at that instant, and there is no way to regenerate only your own work. With more than one person in the tree, any run publishes everyone's mid-edit state, and the coplanar count afterwards describes the whole park rather than your change. On 2026-08-16 that produced a confident report of seven pairs in a scene the reporter had never edited, in work its author was still typing. The only honest way to attribute a finding to your own change is to know the node names your own code emits.

**The rule applies to bodies too, and until 2026-08-14c the test could not see one.** Its first line was `if not type.startswith('CSG'): continue`, and a guest is MeshInstance3D over shared meshes — so it opened both crowd scenes, found nothing in them, and reported zero pairs. That was true and meant nothing. Guest geometry had never been checked by it at all, *including* on the day the heels of every guest in the park were found to be z-fighting by somebody playing the game. The park is 3,228 CSG shapes and 3,021 meshes, and every one of the meshes is a person; it now reads both, in 0.3s. The first run found two fights nobody knew about — a sunhat brim whose top face landed on exactly the crown of the head, white on skin, and a plush toy flush with the sleeve of the arm holding it.

Three things about the extension are worth knowing before touching it, because bodies are not shaped like buildings:

- **Parent transforms are composed.** No CSG node in this park is nested; every guest part is, three and four deep. That is also why ignoring `parent=` was safe for as long as it was.
- **Comparisons happen inside a frame, not in world space.** Every guest is rotated to its own heading, so composing to world would make all 3,021 meshes non-axis-aligned and skip the lot. A node whose own basis is not axis-aligned starts a new frame, and shapes are only compared with others in the same one. Two faces coplanar in a shared rigid frame are coplanar in the world; two in different frames are two different people standing near each other, which is a separation problem and not this tool's business.
- **There are two area floors.** A building's wall and a guest's shoe are both real fights and one threshold is wrong for one of them. The heels bug was 21cm² and plainly visible; the old floor was 200cm², so even with meshes parsed it would have been dropped as a sliver. Meshes are held to 15cm², CSG to 200cm², and a pair is judged by the smaller of the two.

Two older faults surfaced on the way and are fixed: the alignment test rejected any basis whose diagonal was not 1, but a mesh carries its *size* in the basis, so every scaled box looked rotated; and spheres and tori were judged by their bounding boxes, when a sphere resting in a box shares no plane with it and touches at a point. That second one had been in the CSG path from the beginning.

**What it still does not cover is printed on every run** rather than left implicit: how many shapes sit alone in their frame and are therefore never compared (rotated geometry, mostly wheels — a wheel is checked against its own parts, not against the chair it is bolted to), and how many have no flat face at all. It also assumes no scene is instanced at an offset, which is true today and would be measured in the wrong place silently if it stopped being.

**A stand-in has never been compared with the thing it stands in for, because no standpoint can see both.** `_plaza_from_below` and `_plaza_from_the_east` each replace the plaza in the section that has just freed it, and the two sections are mutually exclusive by construction — which is the whole point and also the reason neither copy had ever been looked at beside its original. Every screenshot ever taken of either one is a screenshot of a grey box with nothing to compare it to, and a grey box is exactly as convincing as you decide it is. `tools/_massing_probe.gd` is the check: a **free camera**, held at one position while `ParkSections.enter` swaps the section underneath it, shooting the same frame in `plaza`, `boardwalk` and `terraces`. It has to be a free camera and not the player, because a swap moves the player to an arrival marker and half these standpoints are on ground one of the three sections does not own.

What it found on the first run, on 2026-08-19: from the belvedere and the head of the east climb the plaza went from a skyline of cornices, parapets, cupolas, water tanks, awnings and a working clock to **four blank boxes and a tan obelisk**; from the west overlook, six metres from the reveal, the arch lost its own jamb, valance, thirteen bulbs and two festoon runs, and the plaza's floor came back as a **white sheet** — `far` haze applied to 104m of up-facing brick, which `_plaza_from_the_east` had already diagnosed and fixed on its own side, in a comment that says so in as many words, without anybody carrying it west.

**The fix was to mount rather than to copy, and that is the general rule here.** `plaza_frontage`, `plaza_paving`, `plaza_fountain`, `plaza_props` and `plaza_clock` carry no collision at all or carry it entirely inside the plaza's wall line, which the massing seals — so from either neighbour they are decoration, and a stand-in for decoration is a second description of it. What a stand-in must still own is the **walls**, because that is the one part that cannot be one thing: the near run has to collide and the far runs must not, and no mounted copy is both. So the haze stops at the walls. The wash was always a west-side idea anyway — the promenade is ninety metres out and the belvedere is forty.

Two traps on the way in, both worth knowing before mounting anything else across a seam. **Check what the receiving section already draws**: `boardwalk.tscn` had been laying the terrace's six paving runs itself, correctly, for as long as it did not mount `plaza_paving.tscn` — and the moment it did, those became two identical quads a quarter-millimetre apart on ground the player walks on, which is *under* `coplanar_test.py`'s fifty-micron floor and over the eye's. And **check which direction the duplication runs**: `west_far.tscn` is safe to mount in `terraces` and must never be mounted in `boardwalk`, because that section builds the same wheel for real off the same `_wheel` call.

**`coplanar_test.py` has no notion of which scenes are ever mounted together, and two stand-ins for one object will collide across sections.** `boardwalk.tscn` and `terraces_far.tscn` both stand a massing copy of the plaza's hand-authored boxes, both snap to the same centimetre grid, and identical sources therefore land on identical planes — so the tool reported a real, correctly-computed pair about a frame no player can ever be in, because the two sections are mutually exclusive. The copies are separated by `EAST_FAR_NUDGE` rather than the report being ignored: a permanently-red line is worse than the pair it describes, because the next real one hides behind it. If a reported pair spans two scenes, check whether they can be standing at once before moving anything.

**Two rows a box's thickness apart are one pair seen twice.** The report prints each *face* separately and `EPS` is 0.00005 — fifty microns. So two boxes flush in z at 0.4 thick print as `-Z @ -7.498` and `+Z @ -7.098`, which reads exactly like a single pair comfortably separated by forty centimetres and invites the conclusion that the tolerance is loose. It is not, and nothing at fifty microns is a rounding artefact. Two people reached that wrong conclusion off the same output on 2026-08-16, one of them reasoning from the report's shape without opening the nodes. If a pair looks too far apart to be real, compare the two shapes' extents before dismissing it — the ones in question turned out to share both faces to four decimal places.

**No pointer.** Aiming is look-based — relative motion, no on-screen cursor. Flat interfaces are grid-navigable with directional input.

**Input goes through actions, never raw device polling.** Mobile and gamepad are both open possibilities; keeping input abstracted is what keeps them open.

**No meters, scores, or currencies.** No stamina, hydration, sales targets, photo grading, or film limits. Nothing in the game counts up or down. Collection completion is tracked as filled and empty slots in the album.

**Failure is cheap.** Being caught somewhere off-limits returns the player to a gate. Nothing is deducted.

**Camera capability is the progression gate.** The starting Instamatic has fixed focus and exposure and no zoom, and cannot expose a dark scene. Later cameras open places the player has already walked past.

**NPCs and events run on the park's schedule.** Subjects move through states by time of day rather than idling in place. Some photographs are only available by waiting for a circumstance or causing one.

**The time is read off the park, not off the screen.** `ParkClock` is real and everything schedules against it, but the only readout is the clock face on the sign tower. Knowing what time it is should be a small piece of knowing the park.

The crowd is the second way to read it, and deliberately: how many people are in the plaza and whether the cafe tables are taken should be enough to place the hour within about two either way. Anything else that goes on the schedule should be legible the same way — a thing you can look at and know the time from, not a thing that merely varies.

## Conventions

- `snake_case` for files and directories
- Scenes in `scenes/`, scripts alongside their scenes, shared code in `scripts/`
- Signals over direct cross-system calls
- Autoloads only for genuinely global state
