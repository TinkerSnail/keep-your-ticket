The container port · the mainland shore by the bridge's east end, facing the city · pass 1 · proposal (P) for review

Port Plan

Christina, 3 October 2026: “lets add a modern port near the city”; asked where, she chose the mainland shore by the bridge's east end, across the water from the city, like Oakland facing San Francisco. This pass draws a late-1990s container terminal there: two berths, post-Panamax ship-to-shore cranes on a 30.48 m gauge, a straddle-carrier yard, a truck gate, tugs, and the water the ships need. Measured in world metres over the terrain master as it stood on 3 October 2026 (sha1 04d19c17), x east, z south, north up. A is approved or decided, P proposed, E exists. Point at anything on the map, or tap it, to name it; the readout also gives the ground's height.

Literal-use rule. This is a review diagram, not construction input. Every line here is a review diagram: the port's own, and the other plans' lines redrawn for context. Nothing is to be traced into ParkPlan, a generator, the terrain or a scene. Before anything is built, the quay, the fill and the landform need a Blender source Christina can reshape, and the yard, the gate and the roads named editor-owned sources for placement; code only derives from them.

Drawn and recommended: a terminal on fill off the shore, which keeps all 217 of South Shore's lots; its southern beach then looks across a lagoon 41 to 133 m wide at the terminal.   3. The water is tight. A turning basin of 1.5 ship lengths (477 m) fits only off the quay's north-west end: 42 m from the city's north shore, 43 m from the ferry route, 46 m from a ship at berth.
  4. There is no seabed in the game. The heightfields stop at the water; the master's placeholder shelf gives 2.6 to 11.2 m along the berths. The berths, basin and channel are drawn dredged to 15 m (P).
  5. Her inlet fill and edit 2 overlap. Filled to her line, the inlet would landlock South Shore's beach from z 2075 to 2256. This plan makes that strip a green.
  6. Seen from the park. From the plaza the ground lets only the top 29 m of a raised boom show, and the plaza's own south frontage hides that. From the arrival road they are hidden.

The promise

What the port is for

The coast's working edge, seen from everywhere across the water: six cranes on the skyline beside the bridge, a ship alongside most days with tugs nosing it in, straddle carriers shuffling boxes, trucks queuing at the gate at dawn, the cranes lit at night and their booms raised like a row of drawbridges when no ship is in. It is the city's opposite shore, the reason the bridge carries trucks, and a subject for the camera from the pier head, the beach town, the ferry, the bridge and the city's waterfront. Nobody visits it; it is observable from its fence, the green and the water, and its cranes advertise it over the ridges.

Depth

The berths, the basin and the channel

There is no seabed in the game: the 1 October heightfields find nothing under open water, and the only floor is the terrain master's placeholder shelf (2 m deep at the shore, falling 1 in 50, to 40 m). On that shelf the berths have 2.6 to 11.2 m, the basin 3.5 to 13.0 m, the channel 8.4 to 27.5 m. The ships need about 14 to 15 m at the berth (a 1996 post-Panamax draws about 14 m), so the plan draws the berth pocket (4.6 ha), the basin (17.9 ha) and the shallow half of the channel (12.6 of 42.5 ha drawn) dredged to 15 m, −22.5 m Godot (P). When the seabed is authored, this is its first requirement.

Decisions

Ten for Christina

1 · Where the terminal sits

Recommended: on fill off the shore, as drawn. Rooted on her line and her inlet at the bridge's east end, the quay runs 680 m with the coast and faces the city; every one of South Shore's 217 lots stays. The cost: the southern half of South Shore's beach looks across a 41 to 133 m lagoon at the terminal's back instead of the open bay, and the turning basin is tight. Or: onshore (outlined in red): the same quay on the coast, the yard on South Shore's bench; it takes 78 lots (beach lots S078 to S119 and land-side lots L063 to L098) and about 550 m of beach, and gives the basin about 110 m of room each way. Or: one berth now (outlined in brown): the south berth and its yard only, about 7 ha of quay and stacks with the same landside; the bay stays open in front of South Shore north of about z 1960; the north berth later.

2 · How many cranes

Recommended: six, three a berth, so both ships can be worked at once and the skyline reads as a port from across the bay. Or: four, two a berth, drawn solid here; cranes 3 and 6 are the dashed pair. At about 5,000 triangles each the difference is about 10,000.

3 · On-dock rail or trucks only

Recommended: trucks only. The regional line is a passenger line on a viaduct 15 to 22 m above the port's deck here; a freight spur down to the quay at 2 % would need 0.8 to 1.1 km of its own, and there is no room for it. Or: a short spur and an intermodal yard on the landside, which means a freight connection the rail plan does not have.

4 · The city's old port site

Recommended: the City Peninsula map's heavy working port in the west waterfront blocks becomes city and public waterfront, Harbor Walk carried on round it, and the port overlook at the north-east corner kept, now facing the new port 645 m across the water. Or: keep a small break-bulk or fishing quay there, no cranes.

5 · The quay's top

Recommended: 4.5 m over the sea (−3.0 m Godot): a metre over South Shore's bench and 5 m under the highway at JP. Or: 5 m (−2.5), a little more freeboard and a shorter port road.

6 · The trucks' way in

Recommended: JP, a signalised T on the coast highway at z 2352 where it runs straight and level; left for the towns, right for the bridge and the city; South Shore's J2 and its south link carry no port traffic. Or: a pair of ramps under the highway, which need the green's room. Existing roads do not move in this plan.

7 · The turning basin

Recommended: 1.5 ship lengths, 477 m, as drawn, off the quay's north-west end; about 40 m from the city's north shore, the ferry route and a ship at berth. Or: 1.2 lengths, about 380 m, with two tugs, which adds about 50 m of room on every side.

8 · The tugs

Recommended: a float for two tugs on the lagoon side of the terminal's north end, sheltered and a short run to the basin; it gives South Shore's beach something to watch. Or: at the terminal's south corner by the bridge.

9 · Her inlet in front of South Shore's south beach

Recommended: where her inlet fill would landlock South Shore's beach (z 2075 to 2256), make it the port edge green, planted, the south lookout kept at its north end. Or: stop her inlet line at the beach so that strip stays water, which amends her 30 September line.

10 · The lengthened bridge

Recommended: when its towers and piers are laid out, keep them out of the berth pocket and at least 60 m from a ship at berth 1; the port keeps its fence 51 m off the deck. Or: move the quay 30 m north-west, which takes 30 m from the basin's room.

Where this meets other plans

Conflicts and changes

  • South Shore Plan, pass 3. No lot is taken by the recommended layout, but 26 lots stand within 100 m of the fence and 106 within 200 m, and the southern beach (about z 1700 to 2150) faces the lagoon and the terminal instead of the bay and the sunset. The south lookout and the inlet green are kept; the south link and J2 stay South Shore's own.
  • Her 30 September line. It holds as the shore only south of about z 2075. North of that, edit 2 (1 October) put the bench and its beach west of it, by up to 110 m.
  • Her 30 September inlet. Filled to her line it covers the water in front of South Shore's beach from z 2075 to 2256 (decision 9).
  • City Peninsula map. Its working port in the west waterfront blocks is superseded by her 3 October decision (decision 4). The coastal plan's morning decision “keep heavy port work in the city” goes with it.
  • The rail. The viaduct round the spur passes over the port's landside 15 to 22 m up; the gate and the truck queue stand under it. Its west end, drawn to the built deck at x 151, moves to the lengthened bridge's landing at (461, 2448).
  • The bridge. Its lengthened span has no towers or piers yet (decision 10). The fence is 51 m off it, a ship at berth 81 m.
  • The ferry. The quay is 519 m from its route and the basin 43 m; the channel crosses it once, at 57°, 451 m from the city landing, where ships in the channel have the right of way.
  • The sunset. From the pier head the port lies on 158° to 174°, the basin and channel on 180° to 243°: at least 37° clear of the protected 280°–310° water.
  • The world maps. The detailed world map and the world-beyond map do not show the port yet; this plan does not edit them.

How this was drawn

The ground. The terrain master, assets/source/world_terrain_master.blend as saved on 3 October 2026 (sha1 04d19c17, after edits 1 to 7), copied to a scratch folder and read there with headless Blender; never opened in place, never saved. Its terrain_world was sampled every 5 m over x −1000 to 1100, z 1300 to 2800 by rays from above, and every 25 m over the whole world for the locator. The game's ground is the 1 October heightfields (heightfield_south_coast_10m.csv, heightfield_upray_25m.csv, with the inside-out tops the manifest describes). Heights are Godot metres; the sea is at −7.5.

The bridge from assets/source/far_shore_city_relocation.blend, copied and read headless the same way: bridge_main_deck spans y 27.80 to 30.20, towers to 121 m. Her lines, the inlet, the spit, the lengthened bridge, the city's port site, Harbor Walk, the terminal and the ferry route from detailed-world-map.html (read only) and the master's reference curves; South Shore's lots, streets, waterline, crest and old shore from south-shore-plan.html pass 3; the rail viaduct from the master's PROPOSAL_rail_viaduct_spur; the highway from coast_highway_centreline.

The port's numbers are the boats brief's (3 October): crane gauge 30.48 m, outreach about 50 m, backreach about 18 m, lift about 38 m, hinge about 46 m, apex about 72 m, boom stowed about 100 m, 27 m over the bogies; ships 294 × 32.3 m and 318 × 42.8 m; 40 ft boxes 12.192 × 2.438 m. Chosen here: berths 330 and 350 m, the apron 55 m, rows on a 4 m pitch, the deck at 4.5 m over the sea, the berth depth 15 m, the basin 1.5 ship lengths, the channel 250 m, the bridge keep-clear 60 m.

Sightlines are rays from the eye to each crane over the ground sampled every 5 m, with no earth curvature (the game has none). The South Shore lots counted are those whose outline touches the fence's polygon. Nothing here was checked with a camera; the views are Christina's to judge.
