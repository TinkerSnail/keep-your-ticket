Water first

The lake outlet, river banks and flood margin remove land from the parcel plan. Highway and railway receive separate bridges; local drainage reaches the river through mapped culverts. A dirt Lake Access Road branches from Hill Road and ends at a dry north-shore turnout; it never enters the blue flood margin.

Main street retrofit

The coffee shop, one-screen cinema and small skateboard retail/service counter occupy bays inside existing blocks. The skate counter is not a freestanding kiosk. On both sides the order is building frontage, unobstructed sidewalk, planting strip, parking, then street. Sidewalks remain outside footprints and continue across the bridge.

Upper bench and homes

The red symbol is the character’s existing house, not the town’s only home. Representative houses show the larger built neighborhood continuing along Hill Road, House Lane and South Valley Road. Drive-In Road carries cars from Hill Road; a separate sidewalk reaches its entrance.

Movement

A dedicated footpath follows Beach Lane from the station to Main Street. South Valley Road gives every block below the river a street address and continuous sidewalk. Civic Walk branches to separate church and gas-station entrances; the gas driveway crosses it only at a marked pedestrian crossing. The skateboarder route remains on sidewalks and crossings.

Before Godot

  • Survey lake elevation and prove a continuous downhill outlet to the sea.
  • Set flood margin before accepting any town or drive-in parcel.
  • Resolve independent highway and railway bridge spans, then culverts.
  • Design the station’s controlled pedestrian rail crossing as an explicit editor-owned element.
  • Confirm the drive-in screen reads on the evening commute without cresting the valley silhouette.
