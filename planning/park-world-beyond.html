What this map is. The footprint map's frame stops 240m south of the plaza and cannot hold a pass a kilometre out or a shore two kilometres long, so the world outside the developed envelope is planned here, in the same world metres, at a scale where the park is a small rectangle. Built layers are drawn from ParkPlan's own numbers: both coast outlines and the promontory spine, the crescent range's toe and its named summits and spurs, the highway and its tunnels, the north valley's rim, the three towns' extents, streets and the house, the two beaches, the city's peninsula, downtown plain and hills, the north shore and the south coast, the approach road, the front road, the parking and the developed envelope. Proposal layers are dashed and exist only on this map until Christina has looked at them and a package builds them.

The line through it all is the coast highway, and the commute is a drive down it. Christina's ask on 2026-09-04: a Pacific Coast Highway equivalent, and the reading that if this were Pacifica to Santa Cruz the character would live north of the park and the hills and the tunnel would be there. The park fills the shelf from the water to the range's toe, so a through highway cannot pass it along the shore; it goes behind the first ridge instead, in the trough the range profile leaves at its inner edge, a level saddle about ten metres up with almost no spur relief all the way round. Built the same day, and it is the plan's second alignment. The first looped the second ridge's dip valley 770m out with a crest turnout for a wide view down onto the park, and the generator rejected it: the spurs stand sixty to ninety metres over the valleys between them right down to the foothill, so that loop was seven tunnels totalling 2.7km, and the road could not climb the second ridge at 1:12 fast enough to top it, so the crest was buried. The drive as built, from the north (the town since 2026-09-05): the house thirty metres up the north side of a creek valley, down the lane to Hill Road and the main street, which is the highway across the valley floor; the climb up the valley's south wall and 443m of tunnel; out on the range's shore-fade face 60m inland, forty metres up, forest above and the sea below, with the headland and the lighthouse a speck across the water (Q−3, first sight); a glimpse bend and a second short tunnel where the face is steep; the descent round the arm's foot to 45m inland, where the bay opens with the lighthouse ahead and the tower behind it (Q−2); the trough behind the park, where the park never shows, which is the wait; the junction at the foothill's back, and the park's own road cresting the foothill with the gate, palms, wheel and tower ahead and the parking below (Q−1, the arrival). Far, opening, arrival. What this alignment does not give is a view down onto the park from height: a through road never climbs behind the park, and a high view would be a lookout of its own, not the highway. The same road in reverse is the drive home at close, and the one not taken on the night the player stays. The highway goes on round behind the parking and out to the bay's far shore to the south-east; the park sits on a spur off it, the built approach road, as a beach boardwalk sits off Highway 1.

The headland, as one landform (Trinidad Head proportions). Built 2026-09-04. The lighthouse district's 4m pad and the promontory were two things where the reference has one. Built: the north outline widened into a headland about 240m long and up to 140m wide with the point at (−242, −295); the spine up its middle as one curve with no bend over sixteen degrees; a level top at 30m near the point, not the forty first proposed, because the walk climbs the spine from the pad at 1:7.7 and forty would have been 1:5.5; the crown set by distance to the shoreline with a 20m cliff skirt, dropping to a low floor at the two coves on the north-west face, which the outline's cliff band cuts as before; the feature bounded narrowly through the turn off the pad and widely past it, so it lifts nothing under the park; the lighthouse and the keeper's exhibit on the level top 37m from the tip, about 27m inside the cliffs on both faces, with a forest clearing round them; the walk re-laid up the spine to the forecourt's rim, walked by the controller and standing on its ground; and in the cove's lee a short breakwater with a light at its head, a plank quay with bollards, and two boats. The lighthouse's frames and the drive's Q−2 and Q−3 were re-shot. What is not built: the path down to one cove, which needs 1:8 over a 25m drop on the cliff face and is a walk test of its own.

South of the park. Soft, long and mostly silhouette, since only the pier head, the wheel, the sky ride, the head of the east climb and the aerials look at it. The parking clause's 20m bluff and cove; the beach town and its beach on the shelf (built); then the long shore curving south-east under the hills the south arm brings down to it, still a green shelf with a skirt, where the proposals put a dune-backed beach with one or two sea stacks off it outside the sunset sector. Since 2026-09-05 (Christina: mend the open faces with geology; a peninsula for the city, Singapore or Rio from across a bay at something like San Francisco's scale) the far shore curves over past the beach town and close to the city into a low neck and a peninsula the city stands on, with a flat downtown on its waterfront and two steep hills behind, a sugarloaf rising out of the water at its south-west; beyond the peninsula a south coast of headlands and coves runs east to the world's edge, and the range's south arm comes down to it on a 600m fade. The highway crosses the neck to the city, through a 293m tunnel off the far shore's ridge. The land is an island: an east coast of the same kind, off this map at x 3400–3600, joins the two shores at (3500, 200), the sea reaches twenty kilometres every way, and the start-screen aerials (`a00`–`a03` in the world-ends record) show no edge. The north end is the same: past the valley the coast turns east as a north shore of headlands and coves, three of them cliffed, the range tapering to it on the same long fade so the valley's north wall, five hundred metres south, stands as it did.

The tunnels. Tunnels are derived, not placed: a stretch buried deeper than 9m for more than 40m is bored, lined, given a headwall at each portal and a lamp every 24m; everything shallower is a cutting with an 18m batter. Since the valley (2026-09-05) that is four: 152m at the road's north end where it climbs out of the valley (the road ends at z −2200, inside the hill; the coast itself runs on to the world's edge at z −2400 since the open-face fix of 2026-09-05), 443m from the valley's south wall to the portal at Q−3, 122m at the arm's foot facing the headland, and 98m on the far shore. A fifth, 157m at the far shore's end, went with the open-face fix later the same day: the shore runs on to the world's edge now, the range's south arm fades to it, and the road there is a cutting. The rejected loop's crest turnout, junction tunnel and hill town in the dip are gone; the north town's houses climbing the valley side do the hill town's job. The trough passes about a hundred metres behind the parking's front road, and the first ridge's crest hides each from the other, shot from the lots on 2026-09-04.

The towns, built 2026-09-05, each where the generator said land for a town exists. The map's north town site between the two north tunnels was read off the height functions first and was a 1:1 face into the sea, eight metres up at twenty metres inland and sixty at sixty; the city's site beyond the far headland was 130 to 210m up the range; only the beach town's shelf was flat. So the north town stands in a creek valley cut into the range at the coast's north end: its axis leaves the shore at (−505, −1830) on the coast's perpendicular, 380m half-width at the mouth narrowing to 200 inland, its floor a twentieth of the range's height (3m at the highway, 12m three hundred metres in), sides at 1:6 low and 1:1 high, a forty-metre beach at the mouth. The highway crosses the floor as the main street with shops both sides, a gas station and a church; Second Street behind it, Hill Road up the axis, a lane to the beach and its lot, and the lane that climbs the north side to the character's house, thirty metres up, facing down the valley to the sea. The beach town stands on the shelf south of the parking off a beach road that leaves the turning circle and runs along the shore past two cross streets to a dead end with a lot at the beach's far end; the shore street at z 400 is its way onto the highway, where the highway is still at shelf level, since the ground between the shelf and the highway's line rises at 1:4 further south; shops stand between the two junctions, a thirty-two metre beach lies in front and beach-front houses stand where the rule lets them. The city stands on its peninsula since the open-face fix later the same day: fifty-six towers on the flat downtown by the water, tallest about its centre, and a hundred and eighty-four blocks climbing the rest of the peninsula wherever the ground is under 1:2.5, every one footed on the lowest ground under its own footprint; two hills stand on the peninsula, a 130m sugarloaf at the water and a 70m hill behind the downtown; three kilometres from the pier head. Every building is a face in one merged mesh per town; lots are placed by rule along streets (set back, facing, clear of roads and each other, off the beach, on ground under 1:3); streets are the highway's own ribbons cut into the ground; windows are a lit material that never dims after close, since a town does not shut with the park; the forest keeps off each building by a clearing the scene publishes.
