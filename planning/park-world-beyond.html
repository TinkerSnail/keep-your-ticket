02B · The world beyond the park

world coordinates · 1.8 × 3.7 km · north up

What this map is. The footprint map's frame stops 240m south of the plaza and cannot hold a pass a kilometre out or a shore two kilometres long, so the world outside the developed envelope is planned here, in the same world metres, at a scale where the park is a small rectangle. Built layers are drawn from ParkPlan's own numbers: both coast outlines and the promontory spine, the crescent range's toe and its named summits and spurs, the approach road as far as it exists, the front road, the parking and the developed envelope. Proposal layers are dashed and exist only on this map until Christina has looked at them and a package builds them.

The line through it all is the coast highway, and the commute is a drive down it. Christina's ask on 2026-09-04: a Pacific Coast Highway equivalent, and the reading that if this were Pacifica to Santa Cruz the character would live north of the park and the hills and the tunnel would be there. The park fills the shelf from the water to the range's toe, so a through highway cannot pass it along the shore; it does what Highway 1 does at Devil's Slide and leaves the coast over the hill, and that is what makes the drive. From the north: the house in a small town on the north coast; the highway south under the cliffs, with the sea flashing through the trees at two bends; the north tunnel through the arm's last spur, and coming out of it the bay opens for the first time, far — the headland and the lighthouse ahead, the cove, the park small across the water; then the road turns inland and the bay is gone, up the dip valley behind the crescent in forest for a couple of kilometres, nothing to see, which is the wait; the crest on the second ridge above the approach valley, where the bay opens again, wide, with the park directly below on its shelf and the point beyond; forest again on the switchbacks down; the spur above the junction, tunnel or cutting, and the park filling the frame as the road comes out of it, tower, wheel and coaster lined up under the car, which is the shot the credits end on; the junction, the park road down the valley, the front road, the gate. Far, wide, close: three reveals of one thing at three distances, each after the view has been taken away. What the glimpses show is the sea, never the park. The same road in reverse is the drive home at close, and the one not taken on the night the player stays. The highway goes on up the bay's far shore to the south-east and the distant city; the park sits on a spur off it, the built approach road, as a beach boardwalk sits off Highway 1.

The headland, as one landform (Trinidad Head proportions). The lighthouse district's 4m pad and the promontory are two things where the reference has one. Proposed: the district stands on the headland's back, rising gently from the cove toward the point; the point is the headland's own end, about 240m long and up to 120m wide, cliffed on its outer north-west face with the two coves already in the outline under it, a table on top for the loop and the walk, the lighthouse near the tip above the cliff with one path down to one cove, and the harbour cove in its lee sheltered by a short breakwater with a quay and boats. Nothing above 10m enters the sunset sector, 280° to 310° from the pier head, which the map draws. This is a 02A and 04B change together: the lighthouse, its exhibit and the promontory walk were re-sited on the current spine on 2026-09-04 and would be re-sited again on the widened headland.

South of the park. Soft, long and mostly silhouette, since only the pier head, the wheel, the sky ride, the head of the east climb and the aerials look at it. A low beach past the Fairground and the parking, with Coastal Creek's outfall where package 05A puts it at the Boardwalk's south end; the parking clause's 20m bluff and cove; then a long dune-backed beach curving south-east under the hills the south arm already brings to the shore, with one or two sea stacks off it outside the sunset sector; and a low forested far headland closing the bay's south-east end as a silhouette, with the distant city's skyline beyond it by day and its glow by night. One rocky end per bay: the north has it, so the south gets no cliffs along its length.

The pass, the tunnels and the towns. The built valley road reaches (250, 520) about 28m up at 1:12; that point becomes the junction where the park road leaves the highway. Proposed: the highway climbs on by switchbacks on the valley's east flank, through the spur above the junction, a tunnel of about 80m or an open cutting, aimed at the clock tower either way and to be shot both ways before it is decided, since the north tunnel is the reference's one tunnel and a second may read as the trick repeated, to a crest turnout on the second ridge roughly 80m up with the trees cut back at the turnout, since a view through dense forest is not a reveal; drops into the dip between the second and third ridges, where a hill town sits out of sight of everything in the park; follows that dip valley round behind the crescent about 770m out; and comes down the north arm's last spur to the north coast through the north tunnel, then runs north-west under the cliffs to the north town, which has the house. A local beach road links the front road's turning circle, the beach town on the shelf and the highway. The towns are scenery, not sections: driven through and looked at, never walked in this milestone. Every grade is a target under 1:12 by chainage and every height a target for Godot, to be verified by shooting all three reveals from the car's own camera height before any town exists. One thing to judge in Godot rather than here: the highway round the dip valley enters the east frames from the head of the climb as a distant line in the forest, and a string of headlights in the hills after dark; NT-2's geometry and sightlines are untouched by a road 700m behind it, but the backdrop gains a road.

Frames. Three join Q0 to Q6 in package 06: Q−3, the north tunnel's south mouth looking at the lighthouse across the water; Q−2, the crest turnout looking north-west over the bay; and Q−1, the mouth of the tunnel or cutting above the junction looking at the tower. All three are drawn as cones from the car's eye. The glimpse bends are marked as the sequence they make. Shooting is a frame sequence down the road, not stills, because a glimpse is a moving thing.
