Entrance + Arrival · roads, layout, landscaping and the tidal lagoon · pass 20 · proposal (P) for review

Entrance Landscaping Plan

The approach road, front road and turning circle become one horseshoe: arrive down the valley, pass across the park's face, leave south past the beach town. The parking fields move inside the horseshoe, south of the front road, and the brick boulevard crosses the road at a guarded crossing and carries on between them to a belvedere on the rise. Behind the belvedere, a tidal lagoon at sea level fills the low ground between the fields and the highway, open to the sea under Beach Road, where two rock jetties run out from its mouth, with wildlife on its marsh and mudflats and a small boating centre's paddle boats, kayaks and glass-bottom boat. Its west shore is a public waterfront with a walk, a Victorian bathhouse on a beach of its own, and small recreation areas. Drawn over the mounted world as it stood on 29 September 2026; world metres, x east, z south, north up. P is proposed, A is approved and not yet built, E is built. The idea

A horseshoe with the boulevard down its middle

Arrive down the approach road's valley from the highway. It no longer meets the front road at a hard angle; it swings onto it on a 60 m curve.

Pass across the park's face on the crown, one way, westbound. The drop-off is on the passenger side just before the crossing, and the pick-up is its mirror just after. The crossing is a raised brick table the full width of the boulevard, with a guard's booth facing oncoming cars.

Leave south round a 25 m corner into the head of Beach Road, and out along Shore Street to the coast highway. The turning circle goes. The drive home passes the beach town.

Park inside the horseshoe. The two fields are today's fields turned over the road, so their entries come off the crown's south side. Their aisles open onto the boulevard between palms.

Walk up the axis. The boulevard now starts at the belvedere on the natural rise, about 5.5 m above the road. From there the palm allée, the gate and the clock tower line up: the first view, on foot, on the axis.

Turn round. Behind the belvedere the rise drops about 13 m to the tidal lagoon, hidden from the road and the fields by the ridge. A pergola across the axis, after the one at Lake Merritt, is all the gate sees of it, with a little market under its ends after Vienna's Naschmarkt. Walk under the pergola and the deck on its far side carries you out over the water, coffee in hand: otters in the slough, pelicans on the jetty, the glass-bottom boat going out over the eelgrass below. Turn round at the fence and the clock tower stands in the pergola's middle bay.

Measured, and why it fits

What the ground allows

  • The shelf south of the front road is flat (−0.1 m) to about z 265, then rises toward the highway: +2.9 m at (0, 290), +5.5 m at (58, 291), and a crest of about +5.6 m at (0, 315). The fields are graded level into it, with a planted cut bank on the south and east sides. The belvedere sits on the crest itself.
  • Beach Road's centreline runs at x ≈ −83. The first beach house's east wall is at x −67.0, so the fields are 45 m wide rather than 55 m. That leaves 9 m for a hedge and a row of flowering trees in front of the house backs; the east field matches for symmetry.
  • The railway reserve does not cross the fields. It runs past the gardens, where the halt reserve is unchanged, then down the north-east lobe's west shore below the east field, across the arrival arm at a gated crossing where the arm turns into the crown, and over the lagoon on a timber trestle about 100 m long to the beach town's corner by the boating centre, on its way through the town (Christina, 2026-09-30; see the Beach Town Plan).
  • A tidal lagoon has to sit at sea level, the −7.5 m the Boardwalk's water already uses, so every shore is a bank 7 to 10 m high. The belvedere stands about 13 m above the water. The mouth runs west under Beach Road's bridge, between the first two houses, and out across the beach between two parallel rock jetties about 88 m long (K). Tide gates on the bridge's seaward face control what the sea sends in (Christina, 2026-09-30: "the inlet would have a gate anyway"), as Lake Merritt's do, and keep the water still. A 2 m depth at low tide needs a bed at −10.5 m. That is 107k m³ for the west body, ridge shore and mouth, 44k for the north-east lobe (the approach valley's floor is −0.1 m, so its banks are about 7.4 m), and 100k for the east body, which is kept: its south bank is a steep bluff 14 to 16 m over the water, with a guardrail along the on-ramp above it.
  • The game has no tide yet, and the gates decide how much of the sea's reaches the lagoon (decision 8). Held nearly level, the lagoon needs no tide system: the docks, gangways, fishing float, beach and swim enclosure stay put, and the marsh edge is a permanent shallow fringe. Let through, a tide on the park clock (twelve times real) would cycle about once an hour of play, two highs and two lows a day; the water would rise and fall up to 0.9 m either side of sea level, the mud and marsh edge would bare at low water, the floating docks would ride their gangways, and the herons would come out onto the flats.
  • With the fields gone from the north side, the palm-in-the-footpath and dead-end problems (4, 5 and 6) disappear, and the old fields become arrival gardens.
  • The front-road palm row sat 2.5 m west of the axis (x −22 and 17 either side of it). It becomes eight palms symmetric about the boulevard, with the four field entries between them. The two end palms fall inside the new corners.

GBoulevard and belvedere · the belvedere is approved (A)

  • The brick continues from the crossing to z 293, between 5.45 m planted parkways with openings on both aisles. New palm pairs stand at z 250, 270 and 290, clear of the aisles, with hibiscus at car height between them.
  • A grand stair of about 31 risers up 4.6 m. Either side, instead of a ramp, a winding garden path climbs the planted bank: 2.4 m of decomposed granite, about 104 m at 1:23, flatter than 1:20, so it is a stroll rather than a ramp, step-free for wheelchairs and buggies and without handrails. It loops back round beyond the grass clumps, with a bench halfway up looking over the field to the gate.
  • The belvedere terrace is about 32 × 19.5 m on the crest, with a picture-spot sign at its north edge looking up the axis. Hibiscus trees frame the top of the stair and a low azalea bed runs along the parapet. Oaks on the banks flank the view.
  • A pergola after the one at Lake Merritt, straight across the axis at the terrace's lagoon edge: 32 × 6 m of open timber trellis on cream concrete columns, its beams at 3.6 m, seven bays with the axis down the middle one. From the gate it is the end of the boulevard.
  • A little market under its two 6 m end bays, after Vienna's Naschmarkt: four small stands of dark green painted wood and iron, tin roofs under the trellis, fold-up shutters that prop open as awnings over the counters, all facing the axis: coffee and espresso, cakes and pastries, sandwiches and salads, gelato and lemonade. Together they are an outpost of the park's Overlook Café, the view café on the High Terrace: a taste of the park without a ticket, for the pick-up wait, the town and anyone who walks up. It is a sit-down café with a view, not the beach café on Shore Street. The seating is all outdoors, eight tables in the side bays under the trellis and twelve on the deck, with festoon lights over both at night.
  • Restrooms built into the bank beside the stair's foot, doors on the boulevard at field level, so they serve the fields as well as the market; the stands' store in the bank on the other side.
  • An observation deck on its far side: concrete, the terrace's width and 10 m deep, at the terrace's level, its outer 5 m over the bluff, 13 m above the water. The hardscape kit's iron fence round its three outer sides, four coin-op viewers, a picture-spot sign for the view back to the clock tower in the pergola's middle bay, and the market's twelve outdoor tables, the axis kept clear. The section below checks that view.

JThe waterfront

  • The six beach-town buildings closest to the water give way to a public lawn: the 3rd to 6th on Beach Road's east side, and both on Shore Street's north side. The water came within 0.3 to 0.9 m of three of them.
  • A 3.5 m bluff-top walk runs from the turn of the belvedere's west garden path along the top of the bank, railed at the edge, over the mouth on a footbridge beside the Beach Road bridge, to Shore Street.
  • Below the garden path's turn, a Victorian bathhouse (Christina, 2026-09-30: "an old school victorian lagoon bathhouse") built into the bank: red shingle roofs, white trim and a turret over the water, after the Hotel del Coronado. Its door opens off the garden path, and inside, stairs and a lift go down past the changing rooms about 8 m to a veranda on the sand. In front, a beach built out about 12 m into the lagoon, facing south into the sun, with striped bathing tents, and a timber swim enclosure: a deck on two sides with a diving board, and a rope of floats on the side towards the mouth.
  • Small recreation areas along it. On the bluff top beside the bathhouse, a picnic lawn with three tables and two grills. Between Beach Road and the water, an 8 × 22 m sand playground with a play tower and swings behind a hedge, and a timber fishing dock off the walk. At the corner, a sand volleyball court behind a hedge on Shore Street. On Shore Street's north side, the town's beach café, tacos, breakfast burritos and smoothies to take to the sand and the boats, where the belvedere is the park's sit-down view café; a horseshoe pit on the lawn beside it; and the boating centre, after the one at Lake Merritt. It is two levels built into the bank: its rental counter is on Shore Street, and a stair and a lift go down 7.5 m to its boat store and a floating landing, with the paddle boats berthed on one side and the kayaks on the other.
  • Flowering trees and hibiscus hedging along the road sides; benches along the walk. The bathhouse, bathing tents, swim enclosure, tide gates, play tower, swings, grills, pits, net, floating docks, gangways, beach café, boating centre, paddle boats, kayaks and glass-bottom boat are props still to make.

IThe tidal lagoon

  • Tidal, at sea level (Christina, 2026-09-30: "i want this to be a tidal lagoon", "with fun wildlife", "little paddle boat rental maybe or glass bottom boat or both"). The mouth opens to the sea under Beach Road. Salt marsh and mudflat line the north-east lobe, which becomes the slough up the approach valley; the east body joins it to the lagoon, under the arrival arm's bridge. Eelgrass beds lie in the west body.
  • Wildlife, all ambient and unscored. From the library's planned list: sea otters in the slough, pelicans on the jetties, crabs on the mud, starfish on the landing's pilings, moon jellies, halibut and surf perch. Not yet in the library, and yours to add: harbor seals on rocks off the north jetty's head (moved there from the mouth's sandbar, away from the tide pools, Christina 2026-10-03: "yes add the footway and move the seals"), herons and egrets on the marsh, shorebirds on the flats, and leopard sharks and bat rays over the eelgrass for the glass-bottom boat. In the tide pools at the mouth (K): starfish and sea urchins from the planned list, and, not yet in the library, anemones, hermit crabs, mussels on the jetty's rocks, and small fish, tidepool sculpins and young opaleye.
  • A boating centre, not a marina (Christina, 2026-09-30). Its boats are small rentals that stay inside the lagoon, all from the floating landing below it. Paddle boats keep to a roped calm corner of the west body. Kayaks follow a trail of about 234 m each way: along the south shore, through the narrows and under the bluff, then under the trestle and the arrival bridge and up the slough, to turn round within sight of the otters. No sailing: there is not room for it (Christina, 2026-09-30), and model boats are saved for a feature of their own. A covered glass-bottom boat of about 10 m loops along the ridge under the belvedere and back.
  • Your outline, moved where it met something that stands (the dashed line is your sketch). The north-east lobe's west shore moves from x 84 to x 113, so the rail halt reserve and its line stay on land beside the water. The south-east shore follows the on-ramp's north edge, 3 m off it. The west end comes within a metre of three beach-town houses (0.3 to 0.9 m), which decision 7's waterfront replaces.
  • Three fountains where you drew the trefoils: in the north-east lobe (156, 209), in the east body (107, 276) and in the west body (−35, 339).
  • Two oaks on the hill above the north-east lobe. The planted cut bank below the fields becomes the lagoon's north shore ridge, with the belvedere on its crest.

KThe jetties

  • The mouth runs out between two parallel rock jetties, each about 88 m long, past the surf (Christina, 2026-09-30: "yes draw the jetty", then her photos: "the inlet can look like newport beach", "the tops can be walkable like this"). Each is a rock mound about 8 m wide with a rounded end and a 2.5 m path of packed sand and gravel down the middle of its rocks, no rail, and two benches. The channel between them is about 20 m wide. They keep the mouth open and take the waves off the tide gates.
  • A public path reaches the north jetty from Beach Road between two houses and down the beach; the south jetty is reached from the beach. Both are for fishing, and for the sunset over open sea. A small light on a post marks the north jetty's end.
  • Tide pools on the jetties' inner feet (Christina, 2026-10-03: "yes draw the lagoon tide pools"). Inside the gates the lagoon is held nearly level or gets a muted tide (decision 8), so pools there would barely drain; the mouth, seaward of them, gets the sea's whole tide, and the jetties keep the surf off it. The north shelf, on the bathhouse side, is flat rock about 30 m long and 4 to 5 m wide along the north jetty's foot, from the jetty's root 11 m seaward of the gates. The south shelf, for the beach town side, is about 16 m by 2 to 3 m, set seaward of the north one so the channel never narrows from both sides at once.
  • Their tops fall from −7.1 m at the jetty to −7.7 m at the lip, against sea level at −7.5 m and a tide of ±0.9 m. They are wholly bared for about five park hours round each low water (27 minutes of play) and under 0.5 to 1.1 m of water at high tide; the lip drops 2.8 m to the channel's bed. Ten pools, 0.3 to 0.6 m deep, keep their water when the shelf is bared. Until the game's sea has a tide it stands at −7.5 m: the shelves' inner parts are just clear, their pools full, and their lips awash. The channel stays 14.6 m wide beside the north shelf and 16.7 m beside the south, against 19.8 m between the jetties and 15.1 m across the gates.
  • Steps of 15 risers come down 2.6 m from the jetty paths, set into the rocks with a pipe handrail: two on the north jetty, the first where the path from Beach Road arrives, and one on the south. The shelves are not step-free; the jetty paths look down on them, and so does the footway on Beach Road's bridge, below. The footbridge does not: the road bridge beside it hides the mouth. A tide pool class would meet on the north shelf at low tide, a dozen children and their leaders (Christina's photo, 2026-10-02). The learning center's site is still open and is not placed here. The wildlife is in I.
  • A public footway on Beach Road bridge's seaward side, over the tide gates (Christina, 2026-10-03: "yes add the footway and move the seals"). Beach Road is 5.5 to 6 m between its kerbs here, so its balustrades leave only 1.4 to 1.9 m of the 8 m deck: the footway is cantilevered 2.3 m off the deck's seaward face on brackets, 2 m clear inside a balustrade like the deck's, and doubles as the gate-keepers' walk to the gates. At its north end a 2 m sidewalk on the verge runs 17 m, about 0.5 m clear of the eaves of the house on the mouth's north bank, to where the north jetty's path leaves the road, and a zebra crossing joins it to the bluff-top walk across Beach Road. At its south end a sidewalk runs 14 m to the north end of the beach town's promenade (its B, approved), whose beach stair goes down to the strand and the south jetty's path. It is level with the road, so step-free, and it is the shelves' only step-free overlook. From its balustrade the north shelf lies 12 to 44 m off, 10° to 35° down, west to north-west, and the south shelf 45 to 64 m off, 7° to 10° down, a little south of west.
  • Looking in up the channel from the jetties (after Christina's photo from Long Beach): Beach Road's arched bridge 68 m off, its balustrade about 3° above eye level and its lamps about 6°; the belvedere's pergola just left of the channel, 4.5° up, above the bridge rail; and the range either side rather than behind it, Saddleback about 30° to the left and Kiln Hill about 30° to the right, both 14 to 16° up.
  • They stand south of the pier. From the pier head and the promenade they lie at bearings of about 170° to 196°, well clear of the protected 280°–310° sunset water.
  • The game's sea already reaches here. The map widens west to x −200 to show it, and the world can grow with the plan (Christina, the same day: "the map can be expanded both in planning and in reality").

G · the belvedere, cut along the axis

A market under the pergola, and a deck over the water

Looking east, north on the left, true scale in metres. After the pergola at Lake Merritt, Oakland's own tidal lagoon: a straight colonnade across the end of the boulevard, with a little market under its ends after Vienna's Naschmarkt, an outpost of the park's Overlook Café outside the gate, and all its tables outdoors. Walk up the stair and under the trellis, and the deck on its far side carries you out over the bluff, café tables and all. Turn round at the fence and the clock tower stands in the pergola's middle bay.

The beams miss the tower. From the deck's fence, the eye 1.6 m up, the clock tower's spire top (39.8 m, 355 m off) is 5.3° up and the pergola's north beam is 7.3° up, so the ray to the spire passes 0.55 m under the beam. The fence is the worst standpoint; anywhere nearer clears it by more. An opening crops a view by its depth, not its clear height, so the pergola is 5 m deep and its beams 3.6 m up.

The tower stands in the middle bay. It is 3.2° east of the axis from the fence, and the middle bay's columns stand 7.3° either side of it. Seven bays, the middle five 4 m, put the axis down the middle of one, so no column stands on it; the market stands are 13 m either side, well clear of the view.

From the gate, the pergola is a long level line across the end of the boulevard, 32 m wide, between about 1.4° and 2.8° up over the terrace's edge,. The water behind it stays hidden until you are on the crest.

The deck is concrete at the terrace's level, so it is step-free from the garden paths: 32 × 10 m, its outer 5 m over the bluff on raking struts, 13 m above the water. The hardscape kit's iron fence runs round its three outer sides at 1.13 m. Twelve café tables stand on it and eight more under the trellis, the axis kept clear, and four coin-op viewers along the fence face the lagoon. At night festoon lights along the trellis and in swags over the deck light it on the park's schedule. The glass-bottom boat's loop passes just off its edge. For a straight edge under it, the lagoon's north shore runs straight at z 322 from x −17 to 17, which moves 11 m² of water.

Decisions and approvals

Approved · the departure arm

Christina, 2026-09-30: Beach Road from where it meets the front road to where it becomes Shore Street, and Shore Street back to the highway, are part of the park circuit. 2 · The crown is one-way

Westbound only. Every car passes the crossing once at most, the drop-off and pick-up are both on the passenger side, and the guard faces one direction of traffic.

3 · Fields 45 m wide, not 55

Mirrored at full width, the west field would reach the first beach house's wall at x −67.0. At 45 m each field holds about 15 bays a row, 120 in all, still well above the 42 cars shown.

4 · Palms: pair 6 retires, the front row re-spaces

The pair at z 152 stands inside I5 and I6. The ten front-road palms become eight symmetric ones: all move 2.5 m east, and the two at the ends sit inside the new corners and go.

Christina, 2026-09-30. It is marked A on the map.

Kept · the lagoon's east body

Christina, 2026-09-30. It is 100k of the 251k m³ to dig, and carries the middle fountain. The arrival arm crosses it on a bridge about 61 m long, 11 to 16 m over the water; since pass 18 the railway reserve crosses the lagoon further west, on its way to the beach town.

Approved · the bluff under the on-ramp

Christina, 2026-09-30. The east body's south bank is a steep natural bluff like the lagoon's other shores, 14 to 16 m over the water for about 107 m, with a guardrail along the on-ramp. The ramp stays as built, 3 to 8 m back from the edge, and the water stays as drawn.

Chosen · a boating centre, not a marina

Christina, 2026-09-30. The lagoon's mouth is shallow and passes under Beach Road's bridge about 7 m above the water, so a marina's yachts could not reach the sea, and its moorings, parking and fuel would crowd a lagoon of 1.7 ha. The boats are the boating centre's small rentals and stay inside. There is no sailing, since there is not room for it; model boats are saved for a feature of their own. The centre stands on the waterfront, so it waits on decision 7.

Christina, 2026-09-30, after her photos of a twin-jetty inlet. Two parallel rock jetties about 88 m long, with paths along their tops, in place of a long breakwater along the coast, which would have taken the beach town's surf and, past the pier, crossed the protected sunset water.

Christina, 2026-09-30. On the lagoon's north shore below the garden path's turn, where the water is still behind the gates and the sand faces the sun.
