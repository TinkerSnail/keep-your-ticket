Beach Town

The shore road becomes the public promenade; shops, beach services, pier and skate park are changes to its existing blocks and dead-end lot.

Literal-use rule. Activity routes intentionally follow public edges; they are not new landscaping. No SVG line or coordinate may be traced into generated geometry. Roads, shore, promenade, pier and rail require named editor-owned sources before construction.

Public edge

The promenade replaces the seaward curb condition and meets a continuous town-side sidewalk. Its pier crossing, three beach paths, rinse posts, lifeguard station and bins all land on that public route.

Commercial row

Surf school, surf shop and skate shop retrofit existing bays between Shore Street and Second Street, with doors on the sidewalk. Seafood and fishing-shack bays share existing blocks; service doors and bins face Second Street.

Skate park

The shallow bowl, bank, quarter-pipe, ledge and sitting edge replace one marked bay of the existing south lot. The rest remains legible as parking and the lot keeps its own road entry.

Town movement

Beach Road, Shore Street and Second Street form one network to the coast highway. Sidewalks, controlled rail crossings, the station walk, representative homes and the pier crossing connect without passing through buildings.

Before Godot

  • Choose exact curb ramps and crossings from desired pedestrian routes, not every intersection.
  • Fit the public pier to beach profile, water depth and promenade structure.
  • Confirm back-street servicing for seafood businesses and fishing shacks.
  • Replace the selected lot bay with the skate park; do not preserve hidden parking beneath it.
