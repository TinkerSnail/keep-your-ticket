Beach Town Plan

The coast's ordinary place, one building deep along the water: the strand, the promenade, the pier and the ferry, a row of surf, skate and seafood, a skate park at the town's south end, Beach Road on to the highway past a lot for the beach and the ferry, and the homes: a cottage court behind the row, a terraced hillside of cottages across the highway, old low-rise condos and two motels, and a colony of condos on the widened shelf. Drawn over the town as built on 30 September 2026; world metres, x east, z south, north up. A is approved (the 6 September coastal brief), P is proposed, E is built. The promise

What the beach town is for

The park performs a good time behind its gate; the beach town just has one. It is where the coast's everyday life happens at the water: the dawn surf, the skaters after school, the fish market, dinner on the pier, a walk on the promenade at sunset. It is the place the park's guests drive home through, and the place its staff live. It is worth returning to because it runs on its own clock, the tide's and the town's, not the park's. When nothing is on, it is still a beach. And in the last summer, one storefront in the row has been empty a while.

Zone by zone

Where everything goes

AThe strand

  • Decision 3, settled 1 October: built out. Today a steep bank, about 1 in 2.4, drops from the road at 7 m to a strip of sand about 10 m wide, and south of the terrace straight into the water. The strand (the brief's word) is built out about 30 m into flat sand about 1 m over the sea, about half a hectare of today's water, held at its north end by the lagoon's south jetty and tapering to the shore by the south lot; the bank stays, planted.
  • Dunes with ice plant and dune grass along its upper edge behind sand fencing, broken at two accesses: A1 (stairs, z 372) and A2, the landing terrace (C), step-free by its ramp, with steps and a beach ramp from its plaza down to the sand. The stair at Second Street's axis (A3 in pass 4) would have run down the terrace, so the terrace's steps take its place. Each access has bins at its head and a rinse-off post at its foot.
  • A lifeguard tower opposite the row, and a comfort station built into the terrace's north end under the middle tier: restrooms, outdoor showers and the lifeguards' room, its doors onto the plaza.
  • Contract: inhabitable (walked, sat on, photographed from).

BThe promenade

  • The approved “shore road becomes the public promenade”: Beach Road's seaward curb becomes a 6 m concrete walk behind the beach-town brick wall and its railing, from the lagoon's footbridge at z 354 to an overlook at Second Street's axis. Decision 4, settled 1 October: the curb (Christina: “A is good”). The world map's line on the sand, which started inside the lagoon's channel, is not built; the shore road closed to cars was the other reading, not taken.
  • Mexican fan palms every 12 m, benches facing the sea, the town's teal mariner lanterns. Between Shore Street and Second Street it faces the row across Beach Road; at its middle, across the track, the terrace steps down to the plaza and the pier. It is a hike-and-bike path (Christina: “in general it would be nice if the beach road had a hike and bike sidewalk on the sea side”): a two-way bike lane along the curb and the walk by the railing.
  • Past the Second Street overlook the path carries on, about 4 m and shared, along Beach Road's sea side beside the south green: the shore the two moved houses leave, a public lawn with benches and a picnic table between the road and the track (pass 8). From the green it runs on south along the bluff, 6.5 m inland of the track, past the new lot to a lookout about 200 m south of the town (pass 9; Christina: “with option A we can also extend the hike and bike trail south”).
  • Contract: inhabitable.

CThe landing, the terrace and the plaza

  • Decision 2, settled 1 October. The built ferry landing (z 434–446) stays at 1.8 m over the sea, where the bay ferry boards level, and is shortened to 63 m. The steep bank at its root becomes a terrace in two tiers: from the station's edge on the crest, a flight of six sitting steps down to a middle terrace at 4.4 m, a second flight down to a plaza at the landing's height. The steps face west over the bay to the sunset; a ramp runs along each flight, about 1 in 17, the step-free way down (A2).
  • The plaza reaches 22 to 35 m out, square to the pier: the built shelter, moved 5 m out to the pier's gate, is the ferry's waiting room; the three approved fishing shacks line its north side, the second seafood restaurant its south side with its deck to the sunset, two kiosks flank the foot of the steps, and shade trees stand over benches. Steps and a beach ramp go down from it to the strand; the comfort station is built into the terrace's north end.
  • Contract: the terrace, plaza and pier inhabitable; the shacks, kiosks and restaurant observable (counters and windows), interiors later.

DThe row

  • The brief's commercial row between the two cross streets is the four Beach Road buildings between Shore Street and Second Street, retrofitted rather than replaced: D1 the surf school on the Shore Street corner, D2 the surf shop, D3 the skate shop facing the pier, D4 the first seafood restaurant on the Second Street corner. Signs and display windows are legible from the road; no interiors yet (the brief).
  • Behind them, the everyday town in the existing inner buildings: a corner market (D5), a laundromat (D6) and one empty storefront for lease (D7), the last summer showing quietly.
  • A service yard behind the row, reached by a lane off Shore Street, takes the bins and the dawn deliveries; the restaurant serves from Second Street. Metered parallel parking on Beach Road's inland side; crosswalks at Shore Street, at the terrace and at Second Street.
  • Contract: observable (shopfronts and counters; each door a real threshold so a shop can open later, decision 6), the service yard enclosing.

EThe skate park

  • Decision 5, settled 1 October (Christina: “the skate park is great”): the brief's compact concrete park “on the inland side near the south dead-end lot”, on the two house lots between Second Street and the lot, about 470 m² fitted to Second Street's sidewalk, the foot of the highway's bank and 2 m inside Beach Road's kerb. Its two houses move; where they go is still open.
  • The ground falls about 5 m from the highway's bank to Beach Road, so the park has two levels. An upper deck at 12.5 m, cut into the slope under Second Street with a gate onto it from the street, drops into a quarter pipe on its west face, about 2 m, and a bank on its south face, 2.2 m over 6 m. The floor is at the lot's level, 10.3 m, step-free from the lot and the trail: the 8 × 6 m bowl, 1.5 m deep, is sunk into its west end and a knee-high ledge runs along the south. A line: off the deck, down the bank, along the floor over the ledge, through the bowl and back to the quarter pipe.
  • The sitting edge runs 18 m along Beach Road, facing in with the sea behind, on top of a short planted bank (the floor stands about 2 m over the sidewalk there). Second Street's railing looks down into the park; a back wall up to about 4 m holds the highway's bank. In full sun all afternoon. From the deck's front corner at sunset the sun goes down past seafood 1 over the ferry plaza (V4).
  • Pass 1 had it under the highway's deck, in its shade: there is no deck (the piece named for one is a road slab laid on solid fill), its rectangle ran 4 m up the highway's bank and 1 m onto the carriageway and its corner onto Beach Road, and it drew the lot as its 20 × 20 m bounding box (it is 14 × 16 m). The world map's rectangle would have covered Second Street, six buildings and 8 m of the highway. The skaters' route is the shop (D3), the promenade and the park; surfers prep here and walk the promenade to the break by the south jetty, by the surf school (D1).
  • Beach Road no longer ends in the lot (Christina: “it shouldnt dead end”): it runs on through where the small lot stood, past a new lot of about 30 cars between it and the trail, stalls by the skate park and turnouts facing the sea, about 55 cars in all for the skate park, the ferry and the surfers (“skate park and ferry need parking”, “those parking lots tend to be surfer friendly”). Then it runs flat along the shelf between the rail and the highway's bank to about 120 m beyond the town and climbs at about 7% for 200 m beside the highway to join its southbound lanes, where the highway dips to about 25 m: 313 m in all, meeting the sea-side carriageway only, like the town's two cross streets.
  • Contract: inhabitable; the lot and Beach Road walkable and driven.

FThe corner, Shore Street

  • The town's arrival from the highway and, since 30 September, the park circuit's way out. Its corner with Beach Road widens: the town sign, a phone booth, newspaper boxes, bike racks and the promenade's north landing. The coast bus stops on its north side.
  • Coming down it from the highway the street's end frames the pier and the sea (V3).

GThe rail through town

  • Chosen 30 September (Christina: “yes the trestle is fine, go seaward that sounds cute”). From the park halt the line runs down the north-east lobe's west shore below the east field, crosses the park's arrival arm at a gated level crossing near its corner, and crosses the lagoon on a timber trestle of about 100 m to the town's corner by the boating centre. Town streets are crossed at grade; the coast highway never.
  • Down Shore Street in a raised, planted median (“it can go in the middle of a median”): the street widens from 6 m to about 11 m between kerbs, a lane each way either side, still the park circuit's way out. It falls 11 m to 7 m at 6%, steep but workable for a short passenger train, and crosses Beach Road at the corner on a streetcar-tight curve, gated on both roads.
  • Then between the promenade and the bank to the town's south end, crossing at A1 and three times along the terrace's crest, with the station on the crest at the head of the steps, where the train meets the ferry. At the south end it runs on a shelf at the water's edge, its sea face a rock revetment (chosen); the two houses it was drawn to keep moved inland in pass 8, and the shelf stays, the train along the water below the south green. South of town it follows a coastal shelf about 60 m wide on the highway's sea side, climbs to the lengthened suspension bridge and crosses it into the city, where it ends inside the regional terminal on the central waterfront block, raised level with the bridge deck (chosen).

HThe homes

  • Pass 10 (Christina, 1 October): where the town lives, Capitola's way, small and close and in colour. The six houses on Beach Road by the park's front, between its west field and the sea, move (“the homes in H need to be moved”), as do the skate park's two; the six the lagoon waterfront took are drawn as taken (earlier passes drew them as kept, under the waterfront).
  • The court behind the row keeps its cul-de-sac and becomes a bungalow court of seven cottages, the service yard giving up its east half (“i like the culdesac but we can put some more houses”).
  • The hillside across the highway (“a terraced community on the hills”): Second Street goes under the highway and up to a front street climbing east below the interchange's ramps; two level terrace streets on the 25 m and 35 m contours, about 50 cottages stepping down the slope with decks to the view; a 1950s motor lodge and two 1970s low-rise condo blocks at the front street's foot by the highway; public stairs from Beach Road through a walkers' underpass up the hill's face (it rises 15 m in 25 m there, too steep for the second street). The houses take the range's forest layers on this slope.
  • Contract: enclosing (public faces, porches and lit windows; no interiors); the streets and stairs walkable.

IThe colony

  • The shore south of the town pushed out about 38 m from the track, at the shelf's height, with a beach at its foot (Christina: “we can push the strip of land between the highway and the ocean out further along that whole section of the coast”). A lane in from Beach Road crosses the trail and the track at a gated crossing and runs along the rail's fence.
  • Seaward of it, old condos and townhomes on the water in four colourful blocks, the Capitola Venetian's way, and at the north end by the turnouts a beach motel of two storeys facing the sea (“we need our beach town motels too”).
  • Contract: enclosing; the lane and the beach walkable.

Decisions

Seven for Christina, all settled

1 · The rail through town

Settled 30 September. Through town on the seaward side, in from the north over a second lagoon trestle, down Shore Street in a median; level crossings on town streets, the highway crossed only above or below. Then the same night: a rock revetment past the two seaward houses at the south end (both stay; pass 8 later moved them inland), the suspension bridge lengthened to about 720 m to land on the original peninsula's shore, and the line on the bridge into the city's regional terminal. The line climbs from the town, about 7 m above the sea, to the deck, about 36 m, over some 2 km of shore, the last 750 m on an embankment or viaduct rising about 2%. At the city end the deck lands only about 240 m of track from the terminal, too short to come down, so the terminal is raised: its platforms level with the deck, about 28 m over the street, with stairs, escalators and lifts down (chosen).

2 · The pier's deck

Settled 1 October. One pier, the built ferry landing, kept low at 1.8 m so the ferry boards level, and shortened to 63 m; the bank at its root becomes a terrace in two tiers, sitting steps facing the sunset with a middle terrace at 4.4 m, down to a plaza at the landing's height with the waiting room, the shacks, the restaurant and two kiosks (Christina: “there can be a terrace between the land and pier”, “add another tier”, “the pier can be shorter and the bottom tier can be further out”, “room for shops etc”). The world map's separate 69 m pier is not built.

3 · The strand

Settled 1 October. Built out (Christina: “build it out”): a flat strand at about 1 m, about 30 m deeper than today's strip of sand, held by the lagoon's south jetty and tapering to the south lot; dunes behind sand fencing at the foot of the planted bank. The pass 5 plaza stands at its edge.

4 · The promenade's line

Settled 1 October. On Beach Road's seaward curb (Christina: “A is good”): the 6 m verge between the kerb and the bank's crest becomes a hike-and-bike path (Christina: “in general it would be nice if the beach road had a hike and bike sidewalk on the sea side”): a two-way bike lane along the curb and a walk by the brick wall and railing, with fan palms, benches and lanterns, from the lagoon's footbridge to the Second Street overlook, looking over the track, the bank, the dunes and the strand to the sunset. Not taken: the world map's line on the sand, and Beach Road closed to cars along the row.

5 · The skate park's site

Settled 1 October. On the two house lots between Second Street and the south lot, on two levels (Christina: “the skate park is great”); its two houses move. Not taken: the lot itself, 14 × 16 m and too small for the bowl, and the world map's rectangle over Second Street, six buildings and the highway. Then Beach Road, which ended in the lot (“it shouldnt dead end”): it runs on past a new lot of about 55 cars in all for the skate park, the ferry and the surfers, along the shelf between the rail and the highway's bank and up at about 7% to the highway's southbound lanes, and the hike-and-bike trail runs on south along the bluff to a lookout (“A with new lot if the lot doesnt bump into anything”, “with option A we can also extend the hike and bike trail south”). Not taken: Beach Road turning into Second Street with the lot as a cul-de-sac, and a loop lane up the skate park's east side.

6 · How far in the player goes

Settled 1 October. A walkable town with its shops as windows (Christina: “B is good, we can add more as we decide what is and isnt playable”). Every public place is inhabitable: the streets, promenade, strand, pier, terrace and plaza, skate park, trail, stairs, the hillside's streets and the colony's lane, with people on the town's schedules. The shops, restaurants, shacks and kiosks are observable through real doors, windows and counters, each door a true threshold so a shop can open later. The homes, condos and motels are enclosing. More becomes playable place by place as Christina decides. This answers, for the beach town, the 6 September morning decision (the towns as scenery), which was never answered; the north town and the city are not decided here.

7 · Where the homes go

Settled 1 October. Up the hill across the highway, Capitola's way: a terraced community of about 50 cottages on two level terrace streets off a front street, reached by Second Street under the highway and by public stairs through a walkers' underpass, with a motor lodge and old low-rise condos at its foot; a bungalow court of seven cottages behind the row; and a colony of condos and townhomes on the shore pushed out south of the town, with a beach motel (Christina: “yes add houses to all of these in the plan … think Capitola”, “cottages”, “maybe some old townhomes/condos lowrises”, “we need our beach town motels too”). The houses by the park's front and the skate park's two move up the hill. Not drawn: her second street under the highway, which the hill's face is too steep to take; stairs instead.

The questions every proposal answers

Answered for the town

  • Who is it for? Locals first, from the hillside, the court and the colony: surfers at dawn, skaters after school, families on the strand, fishermen on the pier, old couples on the promenade benches, the restaurant staff. Then day-trippers off the ferry and the coast bus, and park guests on their way home. The photographer passes through on the commute.
  • Why here? It is the only flat ground on this coast at the water (the 5 September site probe), between the lagoon's mouth and the highway's climb, and the shore road was already its edge.
  • Arrive, dwell, leave By the highway down Shore Street, by the ferry at the pier head, by the coast bus at the corner, on foot from the lagoon's waterfront over the footbridge; by car to the new lot at the town's south end, and out south up Beach Road to the highway. People dwell on the strand, the terrace's steps at sunset, the plaza's benches, the pier rails and the skate park's sitting edge, none of them in a through-route.
  • Step-free and emergency The promenade, the plaza, the pier and the sidewalks are level; the terrace's ramp (A2) is the step-free way down to the plaza and the ferry, and on to the strand by the plaza's beach ramp, and also the lifeguard truck's. Beach Road and both cross streets carry an ambulance to any point.
  • Staff, supply, close Deliveries at dawn through the service yard off Shore Street, Second Street for the first restaurant, and by hand truck down the terrace's ramp for the plaza's shacks, kiosks and restaurant. The beach rake goes down the ramp at six. The pier gate is chained after the last ferry.
  • Water, waste, rear faces Storm drains outfall to the strand under the promenade; bins at every access and in the yard. The row's backs face the yard, not the street.
  • What the camera gets V1 to V4 on the plan: the strip one building deep from the pier head, the promenade's palms to the plug, the street's end framing the sea, a skater against the sunset from the skate park's deck. From the strand, the park's wheel and tower stand over the lagoon to the north.
  • Ownership Every line here is a review diagram. Before building, the promenade, strand, pier, accesses and skate park each need a named editor-owned source; Beach Road and Shore Street already have theirs (approach_roads_layout.tscn), and Beach Road's run south would extend its curve there, and the houses are still generator-owned, anchored by distance along Beach Road; the hillside's streets, the stairs, the colony's new land and every new building need their own.
  • Hour, season, after close Dawn patrol and the fish market at six; lessons at nine and two; skaters from three; dinner on the pier at dusk; the neon and the lanterns after dark. Winter: the lifeguard tower shuttered, storm surf, the for-lease sign still there.
  • Contract Decision 6: every public place inhabitable (streets, promenade, strand, pier, terrace and plaza, skate park, trail, stairs, the hillside's streets, the colony's lane); shops, restaurants, shacks and kiosks observable through real doors and windows, built to open later; homes, condos and motels enclosing; the far headlands scenic. More opens place by place.
  • The next move The hillside has room above the upper terrace for more rows; the colony's lane can run on south along the shelf; the shelf south of the new lot, between the rail and Beach Road's climb, stays open too; the footbridge leaves room for the station; nothing here takes the land the lagoon's waterfront or the rail need.

How this was drawn
